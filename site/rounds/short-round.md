# Site round: a beginner's first few turns

One short training on the site, run the way a trainee uses it: the page in a browser, signed in with an invite code, a group chosen, and every word spoken through the page's microphone and voice. Only the microphone and the voice are stood in for; the page, the Worker, its database and the models are the real ones.

It is kept short on purpose, because the site's replies are paid for. It tests the site, not the whole skill: the conversation tests in `measure/` do that.

**Group:** `atheism`
**Most trainee turns:** 8

## The simulated trainee

Give the trainee agent these instructions and nothing else. It must not see the skill or its materials.

> You are Sam, a Christian who has never been trained to talk with atheists. You are training by voice on a website, so say everything as you would aloud: one to three short sentences, no lists, no stage directions.
>
> Answer the coach's questions honestly, as a beginner would. If it asks whether you want background first, say yes, briefly. Once, ask the coach a short question of your own about what it just taught.
>
> If a practice conversation starts, answer the person you are talking to in a sentence or two, and once ask them, "What makes you say that?"
>
> Stay polite. Never mock.

## What the grader checks

The grader sees only the transcript and these statements.

1. Every reply is fit to be spoken aloud: no headings, bullet points, tables, links, bracketed citations or stage directions.
2. No reply would take more than about two minutes to say aloud.
3. Each reply follows from what the trainee just said; when the trainee asks a question, the reply answers it.
4. The coach treats Sam as a beginner: it explains the approach before it tests him.
5. If a practice conversation begins, it is always clear who is speaking, the coach or the atheist, and the atheist never coaches.
6. No reply shows the site's workings: no note meant for the system, no "Site note", and no talk of routers, models, prompts, files or tokens.
