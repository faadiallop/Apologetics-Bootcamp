# Jehovah's Witnesses (The Bread of Life)

Status: draft

Source: skill stamp 1350c1da

## opening-beginner

Before we begin, take a moment to pray.

Here is the method. We ask questions instead of making speeches, so the other person's own scripture does the work. Christ silenced his opponents with questions, and Greg Koukl's book Tactics trains the same skill.

Here is the argument in one sentence. Jesus says no one has life without eating his flesh and drinking his blood, and he told all his disciples to eat and drink; yet the Watchtower tells almost every Jehovah's Witness not to, so it keeps millions from what Christ says they need for life.

Why this approach? Jehovah's Witnesses are well trained to answer the usual debates, such as the Trinity, the divine name and the 144,000, and those talks often end in a standoff over translation. This approach stays with Jesus' own words, in their own New World Translation, about something every Jehovah's Witness has seen: the yearly Memorial, where nearly everyone passes the bread and wine on. It needs no Greek and no church history, it does not attack their leaders, and it ends in Christ's invitation to come and eat.

Here is what will happen. First come short lessons, each checked with one question. Then come practice conversations in which I play a Jehovah's Witness, with quick coaching after each, and most of the practice goes to the parts of the method you find hardest, taken in the order Koukl teaches them. Once the method is sure, you'll learn the gospel and how to cross to it; then come real conversations, and at the end you teach someone else.

Would you like to begin with some background on who Jehovah's Witnesses are, just what bears on this approach?

## background-1

say: The Jehovah's Witnesses grew from Charles Taze Russell's Bible Students in the 1870s, and took their name in 1931 from Isaiah forty-three, verse ten. In 2025 about 9.2 million were active in preaching.

check: Where did the Jehovah's Witnesses come from?

expect: From Charles Taze Russell's Bible Students, in the 1870s; the name came in 1931. The gist of dates and figures is enough.

## background-2

say: A small Governing Body at their headquarters decides what the Bible means, and since 2013 they identify it as "the faithful and discreet slave." So in conversation, ask what the Bible says, not what the leaders say.

check: Who decides what the Bible means for a Jehovah's Witness?

expect: The Governing Body, their small leadership at headquarters. A good answer may add that we ask what the Bible says, not what the leaders say.

## background-3

expect: His own, the New World Translation.

expect: To live forever on a paradise earth; only 144,000 go to heaven.

## argument-1

say: Here is the argument in one sentence. Jesus says no one has life without eating his flesh and drinking his blood, and he told all his disciples to eat and drink; yet the Watchtower tells almost every Jehovah's Witness not to, so it keeps millions from what Christ says they need for life.

check: What does the argument say the Watchtower keeps millions from?

expect: From what Christ says they need for life: eating his flesh and drinking his blood, which he told all his disciples to do.

## argument-2

say: The argument has three premises. John six carries the weight, and the Supper texts show where Christ gives what John six says all need.

check: Which passage carries the weight of the argument?

expect: John 6; the Supper texts show where Christ gives what John 6 says all need.

## argument-3

say: The first premise is that feeding on Christ's flesh and blood is necessary for everyone's life. Jesus says, "Except ye eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, ye have no life in you." That is John six, verse fifty-three.

check: What does Jesus say about someone who does not eat his flesh and drink his blood?

expect: He has no life in him. The substance of the words must be right; the reference is not required.

## argument-4

say: Jesus says the same thing the other way round: "Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, hath eternal life; and I will raise him up at the last day." Such a one, he says, "dwelleth in me, and I in him."

check: What does Jesus promise the one who eats his flesh and drinks his blood?

expect: Eternal life, and that Jesus will raise him up at the last day; he dwells in Christ and Christ in him. Any one of these, stated rightly, is enough.

## argument-5

say: And this bread is not for one class. Jesus says, "The bread that I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world." It is for the world.

check: For whose life does Jesus give his flesh?

expect: For the life of the world, not of one class.

## argument-6

say: Their own literature agrees on this point. The Watchtower grants that the life Jesus speaks of in John six is open to everyone who gains everlasting life.

check: What does the Watchtower grant about the life Jesus speaks of in John six?

expect: That it is open to everyone who gains everlasting life, not only the anointed.

## argument-7

say: The second premise is that at his table Christ gives his body and blood to all his disciples and tells them all to eat and drink. He said, "Take, eat; this is my body… Drink ye all of it; for this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins."

check: At the Supper, whom did Jesus tell to drink?

expect: All of them: "Drink ye all of it."

## argument-8

say: In Luke, Jesus adds, "This do in remembrance of me." And he calls the cup "the new testament in my blood," that is, the new covenant.

check: What does Jesus call the cup?

expect: The new testament, or new covenant, in his blood. A good answer may add his command, "This do in remembrance of me."

## argument-9

say: Paul says the cup and the bread are "the communion of the blood of Christ" and "the communion of the body of Christ." And he adds, "we are all partakers of that one bread."

check: According to Paul, who partakes of the one bread?

expect: All of us, all believers; the cup and bread are the communion of Christ's blood and body.

## argument-10

say: The same words join John six to the Supper: eat and drink, flesh or body, and blood, given for others. In John six, verse fifty-one Jesus says he will give his flesh, and at the Supper he gives it.

check: What does Jesus promise in John six that he does at the Supper?

expect: He promises to give his flesh, and at the Supper he gives it; the same words of eating, drinking, body and blood join the two.

## argument-11

say: This second premise does not depend on John six being a description of the Supper. Even if John six speaks of feeding on Christ by faith, the table is where Christ gives every disciple the sign of that feeding.

check: If John six speaks of feeding on Christ by faith, does the second premise still stand?

expect: Yes. Christ still told every disciple to eat and drink, and the table is where he gives the sign of feeding on him.

## argument-12

say: The third premise is that the Watchtower forbids nearly every Jehovah's Witness to eat and drink. In 2025, of more than twenty million at the Memorial, all but about twenty-four thousand passed the emblems on: about ninety-nine point eight eight percent.

check: Roughly what share of those at the Memorial in 2025 passed the emblems on?

expect: Nearly all, about 99.88 percent, or all but about twenty-four thousand of some twenty million. The gist is enough.

## argument-13

say: Their own teaching says the cup stands for a share in the new covenant. So by refusing it, a Jehovah's Witness declares he has no part in the covenant whose blood is poured out "for the remission of sins."

check: What does a Jehovah's Witness declare when he passes the cup on?

expect: That he has no part in the new covenant, whose blood is poured out for the forgiveness of sins.

## argument-14

say: So the Watchtower keeps millions from the table where Christ gives what he says all need for life. It teaches them to declare, once a year, that his covenant of forgiveness is not for them.

check: What does the Watchtower teach them to declare once a year?

expect: That Christ's covenant of forgiveness is not for them.

## argument-15

say: The covenant in the cup is for all God's people. God promises in it, "they shall all know me, from the least of them unto the greatest of them."

check: Whom does the new covenant include, by God's promise?

expect: All God's people, from the least to the greatest.

## argument-16

say: At the door, have them read from their own Bible, and ask your questions in order. Begin with the Memorial: "At the Memorial, did you eat the bread and drink the wine? Why not?"

check: What is the first question you ask?

expect: Whether he ate the bread and drank the wine at the Memorial, and why not.

## argument-17

say: Then go to the texts. Ask, "In Matthew twenty-six, verse twenty-seven, who is the 'all'? Did Jesus add a condition?" Then ask, "In John six, verse fifty-three, what does Jesus say about life for someone who does not eat and drink?"

check: What do you ask about Matthew twenty-six, verse twenty-seven?

expect: Who the "all" is, and whether Jesus added a condition.

## argument-18

say: Next ask, "What does eating mean there?" Their literature says it means faith. That sets up the question that exposes the flaw: "If eating means believing, and you believe, what keeps you from eating at the Memorial?"

check: Once he says eating means believing, what do you ask?

expect: If eating means believing, and he believes, what keeps him from eating at the Memorial. His own wording is fine if it sets the two claims side by side.

## argument-19

say: Ask the question that exposes the flaw last, after he has read the texts and stated his view, so he cannot say that's not what he believes. Ask it once, plainly and gently, then wait in silence, and don't answer it for him.

check: What do you do right after you ask it?

expect: Wait in silence; don't answer it for him or talk on.

## argument-20

say: A second such question sets Christ's command against the Watchtower's rule: "At the Supper Jesus said, 'Drink ye all of it.' At your last Memorial, who told you not to?" Any answer names an authority other than Christ overruling Christ.

check: Why does every answer to that question cost him?

expect: Whoever he names is an authority other than Christ overruling Christ's command to drink.

## argument-21

say: He may answer that those words were written to the anointed, and he is not anointed. Then ask how he tells which words are for the anointed only. Jesus told the apostles to teach every disciple "to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you," and Paul gave the bread and cup to the church at Corinth "with all that in every place call upon the name of Jesus Christ our Lord."

check: When he says those words were written only to the anointed, what do you ask?

expect: How he tells which words are for the anointed only. A good answer may add that Jesus told the apostles to teach every disciple to observe all he commanded, and Paul gave the bread and cup to all who call on Christ.

## argument-22

say: A third question asks about forgiveness: "The cup is 'my blood of the new testament,' the new covenant, 'which is shed for many for the remission of sins.' If you're not in that covenant, by what blood are your sins forgiven?" If he says by the covenant's blood, then he belongs at its table, and there is no other blood.

check: Why does either answer to the blood question cost him?

expect: If he is forgiven by the covenant's blood, he belongs at its table; and there is no other blood that forgives.

## argument-23

say: He may answer that the great crowd come to God through the anointed without a covenant, as the ten men take hold of a Jew's skirt in Zechariah eight, verse twenty-three. But the ten men say, "We will go with you: for we have heard that God is with you," so they go with him to God, and the strangers who join the LORD take "hold of my covenant." Then ask the blood question again.

check: In Zechariah eight, where do the ten men go?

expect: With the Jew to God; they are not left outside. A good answer may add that the strangers who join the LORD take hold of his covenant, and that you then ask the blood question again.

## argument-24

say: A fourth question asks about the facts: "Before 1935 almost everyone at the Memorial ate. In 1935, did the Bible change, or the teaching?" If the teaching changed, then men changed who may eat, for the Bible did not change.

check: What does it mean if he says the teaching changed in 1935?

expect: That men, not the Bible, changed who may eat.

## tactic-gather

say: The first tactic is to gather. Whenever a Jehovah's Witness uses a loaded word such as "anointed" or "partake," or states his view loosely, ask, "What do you mean by that?" so you neither misunderstand nor misrepresent him. With this group, start from the Memorial: "What happens at the Memorial? Did you eat and drink? What would it mean if you did?"

check: When a Jehovah's Witness uses a word like "anointed," what do you ask?

expect: What he means by it, before answering; any question that draws out his meaning in his own words counts.

## tactic-burden-of-proof

say: The second tactic keeps the burden of proof on whoever makes a claim. When a Jehovah's Witness makes a claim, ask, "How did you come to that conclusion?" so he gives reasons, not only opinions. When he says the emblems are for the anointed only, ask, "Where does Jesus or Paul say that only some disciples may eat and drink?" or "How does someone know he is not anointed?"

check: When he says the bread and wine are only for the anointed, what do you ask?

expect: For his reasons: where Jesus or Paul limits who may eat, or how someone knows he is not anointed. A soft "Are you sure?" that asks for no reasons does not count.

## tactic-rescues

say: Two rescues keep you from carrying a load that isn't yours. If he shifts the burden onto you when you have made no claim, say, "I've only asked questions so far. Your view is the one on the table." If you are outclassed, say by an elder pressing the Watchtower's covenant arguments, switch from persuading to fact-finding: ask him to slow down and explain his view and reasons, take notes, and close with "Let me think about it."

check: If an elder outclasses you with the Watchtower's arguments, what do you do?

expect: Switch to fact-finding: ask him to explain his view and reasons, take notes, and say "Let me think about it"; not bluff, give up, or take on a burden he never claimed.

## tactic-staying-on-topic

say: When a Jehovah's Witness raises a side issue, such as the 144,000 or Michael the archangel, keep to the one question: did Jesus restrict who may eat and drink? Let a passing jab pass and simply ask again; park a real question in one sentence, "Can we come back to that once we've finished John six?"; and if he keeps drifting, narrate: "I asked who may eat, and we've moved to the 144,000. Could we finish that first?" Whichever way you take, return to the question by name, and don't debate the number.

check: If he keeps drifting to the 144,000, what do you say?

expect: Narrate: name the question he dropped and where the talk has gone, and ask to finish the first one. Not debating the number.

## tactic-steamroller

say: When an elder keeps interrupting and piling on, take three steps, one for each new interruption. At the first, stop him, "I'm not quite finished"; at the next, shame him with a courteous question, "Do you want an answer, or an audience?"; at the one after, give him the last word and leave kindly, with no jab. If a senior publisher ends the visit with "You're entitled to your opinion, and we're entitled to ours," say, "We can't both be right," and leave a Gospel of John.

check: What are the three steps, and when does each one come?

expect: Stop him at the first interruption, shame him gently at the next, and leave at the one after, with kind words and no jab; none early or skipped.

## tactic-lead

say: Once he has stated his view and his reasons, lead him with questions, each a step he can agree to, instead of making the point yourself. Have him read Matthew twenty-six, verse twenty-seven and John six, verse fifty-three aloud in his own Bible, then ask, "Who is the 'all' in Matthew twenty-six, verse twenty-seven?" and "In John six, verse fifty-three, what does Jesus say about life for someone who doesn't eat and drink?" The aim is that he says who the "all" is, in his own words.

check: What is the aim of your leading questions with a Jehovah's Witness?

expect: That he himself says, in his own words, who the "all" is whom Jesus told to drink; the point comes from his answers, not from our statements.

## tactic-turnabout

say: Sometimes a Jehovah's Witness leads you with questions toward a point he hasn't stated, or makes a claim in the form of a question. Their model door talk asks, "Could it be that some people—some good people—will not go to heaven?" Don't answer the trap; ask him to state his point, "What are you getting at?", and then bring the talk back to who may eat.

check: When he leads you with a question like that, what do you do?

expect: Not answer the trap, but ask him to state his point, then bring the talk back to who may eat.

## tactic-sibling-rivalry

say: Sibling Rivalry is two of his own claims that cancel each other. A Jehovah's Witness holds that the life in John six is for everyone, and that the emblems are only for the 144,000. Set them side by side with one question, then wait: "Jesus says, 'Except ye eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, ye have no life in you.' Your Watchtower says that life is for everyone. So must you eat, or do you have life without it?"

check: Which two of his claims pull against each other?

expect: That the life in John 6 is for everyone, and that the emblems are only for the 144,000. Described truly; the name of the flaw is not required.

## tactic-taking-the-roof-off

say: Taking the Roof Off follows a view through to see where it ends. When an elder says the other sheep aren't in union with Jesus as members of his body, remember that Jesus says the one who eats "dwelleth in me, and I in him," and that without eating "ye have no life in you." Then ask, "Then where is your life?"

check: If the other sheep are not in union with Jesus, what question shows where that ends?

expect: "Then where is your life?" or its sense: Jesus makes union with him the mark of everyone who has life, so outside that union there is no life.

## tactic-unfalsifiable

say: An unfalsifiable claim is one no result could ever count against. When partakers were falling, the Watchtower said that confirmed its teaching; now that they are rising, it puts that down to mistakes or emotional problems. When a Jehovah's Witness says more partakers is no cause for concern, ask, "What result would ever count against the teaching?"

check: Why can no result count against this teaching?

expect: Falling numbers were said to confirm it, and rising numbers are put down to mistakes or emotional problems, so either result is taken as support.

## tactic-just-the-facts

say: Just the Facts catches a claim built on wrong facts. A Jehovah's Witness may say that most of the early church was anointed, yet Paul rebuked the Corinthians for division and drunkenness at the table and still told them all, "Let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of that bread, and drink of that cup." So ask, "How did Paul know every one of them was anointed?"

check: What question checks the claim that the early church was mostly anointed?

expect: How Paul knew every one of the Corinthians was anointed, since he told them all to examine themselves and eat; any question that tests the facts against the text counts.

## their-answers-1

say: When he says, "The emblems are only for the 144,000," remember that no text limits the table. Jesus said, "Drink ye all of it," and the covenant in the cup is for all God's people, least to greatest. To be outside the covenant is to be outside its forgiveness.

check: What does it mean to be outside the covenant?

expect: To be outside its forgiveness. A good answer may add that no text limits the table and Jesus told all to drink.

## their-answers-2

say: When he says John six was spoken a year earlier to a crowd in Galilee, so it isn't about the Memorial, remember that the same article grants the life in John six is for everyone. Jesus said he will give his flesh, and he gave it at the Supper. And even without John six, Christ told every disciple to eat and drink.

check: What does that same article grant that answers its own point?

expect: That the life in John 6 is for everyone; so everyone needs to feed on Christ.

## their-answers-3

say: When he says John six means faith, not literal eating, agree that we feed on Christ by faith. But faith obeys Christ's "This do in remembrance of me," and Paul says we proclaim his death by eating and drinking.

check: If eating in John six means faith, why should a believer still eat at the table?

expect: Because faith obeys Christ's command to do this, and eating and drinking is how we proclaim his death.

## their-answers-4

say: When he says, "I'm not worthy," remember that Paul warns about the manner of eating and assumes everyone eats. He says, "Let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of that bread, and drink of that cup." The cure for unworthiness is to examine yourself, repent, and then eat.

check: According to Paul, what does a man do before he eats?

expect: He examines himself, and so eats; the cure for unworthiness is self-examination and repentance, not staying away.

## their-answers-5

say: Some say only the anointed have the Spirit's assurance that they are God's children, from Romans eight, verse sixteen. But Paul says, "as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God," and that anyone without the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him at all.

check: According to Paul, who are the sons of God?

expect: As many as are led by the Spirit of God; and anyone without the Spirit of Christ is not his at all.
