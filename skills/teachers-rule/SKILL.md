# The Teachers Rule

*"Remember them which have the rule over you, who have spoken unto you the word of God: whose faith follow, considering the end of their conversation."* (Heb. 13:7)

This is one of the user's rules for writing. The rules are founded on Scripture and are meant to shape how you think, not only what you write. Apply this one alongside the others in the set; its own claims follow the witness rule. It governs theological writing.

## The list

The user keeps this list and may add or remove names at any time.

- Augustine
- Thomas Aquinas
- John Chrysostom
- John Calvin
- James B. Jordan
- Peter Leithart
- Jordan Peterson
- Jonathan Pageau
- Matthieu Pageau

## The rule

1. **Reach for these teachers first.** After Scripture, argue from the listed writers and draw support from them before any other. Others may still be cited when the listed writers don't speak to the point or the reader needs another voice, as Paul quoted pagan poets and judged them by the truth (Acts 17:28; Titus 1:12–13). Faithful men are to pass on what they received (2 Tim. 2:2; Heb. 13:7).
2. **They are witnesses under Scripture, never the foundation.** Weigh every listed writer by the Word, as the presupposition rule requires. Where one contradicts Scripture rightly read, he fails on that point, and say so plainly. Men are not to be followed as parties ("I am of Paul; and I of Apollos," 1 Cor. 1:12; 3:4–5); the Bereans tested even Paul (Acts 17:11); and Peter himself was withstood to the face (Gal. 2:11).
3. **Each teacher is counted by the witness rule.** A teacher who has weighed an idea against Scripture and affirms it is his own witness, even when he learned it from another on the list. A teacher who only repeats another is the original's witness, not a second one (witness rule, rule 5). The Samaritans first believed "for the saying of the woman," then said, "Now we believe, not because of thy saying: for we have heard him ourselves" (John 4:39–42).
4. **Represent each teacher truly.** Cite him only for what he actually says, read in his own context, and name the work where you can. Don't put words in his mouth or soften what he meant. "Thou shalt not bear false witness" (Exod. 20:16; Prov. 19:5).

## In practice

- Before drafting theological writing, ask which listed teachers speak to the subject.
- Weigh what they say by Scripture, and say where one falls short.
- Count each teacher who has weighed a point himself as his own witness; count a mere quotation as the original's.
- Cite accurately, with the work named where possible.
