# The Chiasm Rule

*"I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again, I leave the world, and go to the Father."* (John 16:28)

This is one of the user's rules for writing. The rules are founded on Scripture and are meant to shape how you think, not only what you write. Apply this one alongside the others in the set; its own claims follow the witness rule.

Prefer chiastic structure: a piece whose second half mirrors its first in reverse, either around a center (A B C B′ A′) or without one (A B B′ A′). Scripture uses both. Odd chiasms with a center include the flood account (Gen. 6–9) and Psalm 67. Even chiasms include Psalm 1:6, Psalm 19:1 (in the Hebrew word order) and Mark 2:27.

## The ground

**Chiasm is the shape of God's own motion.** This is the framework of the user's thesis, *The Genealogy of the Cosmos*, which holds that the motions of God, and so of the cosmos, are "fundamentally chiastic." The Father gives the Spirit to the Son, and the Son through the Spirit returns himself to the Father: "God giveth not the Spirit by measure unto him" (John 3:34), and Christ "through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God" (Heb. 9:14). Christ gives his whole mission the same shape in the epigraph above (John 16:28): came forth from the Father (A), come into the world (B), leave the world (B′), go to the Father (A′), with the hinge at the seam between the two halves.

**Writers prefer the form because they make their work after God's pattern.** "Look that thou make them after their pattern, which was shewed thee in the mount" (Exod. 25:40, quoted in Heb. 8:5), and "Be ye therefore followers of God, as dear children" (Eph. 5:1).

## The rule

1. **Use the fullest form the material honestly allows.** Write a full chiasm where the material mirrors. Where it doesn't, at least close the ring by ending where you began. Where even that would be forced, write straight through. Never pad, force or distort a point to complete the symmetry. Scripture closes rings (Ps. 8:1, 9; Eccl. 1:2; 12:8), but it also builds straight lists and acrostics (Gen. 5; Ps. 119).
2. **Put the turning point at the hinge.** In an odd chiasm, the hinge is the middle element. The flood account turns on "God remembered Noah" (Gen. 8:1; Wenham and Anderson both find it at the center), and Psalm 67 turns on verse 4. In an even chiasm, the hinge is the seam between the halves. Isaiah 6:10 turns from "shut their eyes" to "lest they see with their eyes," and Mark 2:27 turns at "and not."
3. **The return may repeat, vary or reverse.** A mirrored element can come back word for word (Ps. 67:3, 5; Ps. 118:1, 29), changed (Gen. 2:4; Ps. 99:5, 9), or reversed (Gen. 9:6; Matt. 23:12). Repetition frames and emphasizes; variation and reversal develop.
4. **It must read clearly to someone who never notices the chiasm.** Most readers won't see the structure, so it has to guide them without being seen. The flood account reads as a plain story even though it is a 31-item palistrophe (Wenham), and English translations flatten the reversed Hebrew order of Psalm 19:1.
5. **Chiasms may nest.** A chiastic piece can hold chiastic sections or sentences. Genesis 2:4 is a small chiasm that also closes the larger ring opened in Genesis 1:1 (Wenham), and Mary Douglas lists "rings within rings" among the marks of ring composition. Nest where the material invites it, not at every level by rule.
6. **Lists and acrostics may sit inside a chiastic frame.** Matthew 1:1 names "Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham," and the genealogy that follows runs from Abraham through David to Christ (Matt. 1:2, 6, 16), so the list mirrors the header. Genesis 10:1 names "Shem, Ham, and Japheth," and the table of nations takes them up as Japheth, Ham, then Shem (Gen. 10:2, 6, 21). Scripture also builds straight lists and acrostics (Gen. 5; Ps. 119), so the frame is permitted where the material mirrors, not required.

## In practice

- When planning, look first for the piece's frame and its hinge.
- Outline as A B C B′ A′ or A B B′ A′ if the material mirrors; otherwise close the ring, or write straight through.
- Before finishing, read it as someone who doesn't see the structure, and cut anything added only to complete the symmetry.
