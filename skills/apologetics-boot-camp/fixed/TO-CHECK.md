# For the author to check before approving

Each group's file was drafted by a model from the skill and the group's SOP files. The writers listed the places below where the material was thin, where they chose the wording or the placement themselves, or where they were unsure the wording is faithful. Strike a point when it is settled, and remove a group's section when its file is approved.

Three kinds of point recur in every group:

- **Questions the writer built** where the material records none.
- **Where the flaw questions and their escapes sit.** The skill teaches them with the method, but a tactic's teaching is held to 100 words and one example, so the writers put them among the argument pieces or the answers.
- **Sentence counts.** A piece that quotes a member's questions inside one sentence was counted as that one sentence.

## Muslims (`islam.md`)

1. `tactic-suicide`: the material gives no question for this flaw with Muslims. The piece pairs "Can a book confirm another book and deny its central event?" with the Qur'an's own test that a book not from God would hold much contradiction.
2. `tactic-taking-the-roof-off` and `tactic-infanticide`: both questions are the writer's, each joining the method's sample question to its Islam example ("If that's true, wouldn't it also follow that no eyewitness can be trusted, including those who passed on the Qur'an?").
3. The recorded escapes to the exposing questions are in `their-answers-11` to `14`: the lost Injil or the one hidden until the Mahdi, that Jesus survived the cross, the infallible Imam, and "a data point."
4. The short version's fifth question ("Will you read Luke with me?") is left to the bridge pieces, since the gospel is held back until then.
5. Qur'an references are spoken as "chapter five, verse forty-seven." The material doesn't say how to read them aloud, and "surah" is never used.
6. `tactic-turnabout`: "Is that view incoherent, yes or no?" is given as the member's words, but the response bank only paraphrases him there.
7. `tactic-steamroller`: "the end of Mark and two Injils" is the writer's plain wording of the bank's "Mark 16 and 'Injil-1 vs Injil-2'".
8. Wording that is the writer's sense of the material:
    - `foundation-5`: the line about not wrestling against flesh and blood is reworded from the skill, not a King James quotation.
    - `argument-13`: gives Matthew three and John one as the Gospels' testimony to the Son without quoting them; the material lists the references but quotes neither.
    - `argument-15`: "Jesus taught us to pray to our Father" stands for the material's reference to Matthew six, verse nine.
    - `know-them-4`: Frost is introduced as "Christopher Frost, whose book this approach follows."
9. `argument-12` to `17` teach all seven rows of the contradictions table; the training plan asks for only three.
10. `know-them-10` is a summary the writer added so the trainee can state the Muslim's view. It adds nothing new.
11. Placement: "count the cost" is in the gospel pieces; prayer and friendship are in the bridge pieces; answers to invitations (bank rows 29 and 30) are `bridge-5` and `bridge-6`.

## Jehovah's Witnesses (`jehovahs-witnesses.md`)

1. The training plan's Foundation check (the argument in a sentence, the terms, about 1 in 840, nearly all ate before 1935) is spread across the know-them and argument pieces. The foundation pieces hold only the Scripture and how to carry yourself.
2. The sixth question ("If he invites you, who has the right to tell you no?") is in `bridge-6`, not with the other questions.
3. The four exposing questions and their recorded escapes (bank rows 30 and 32) are in `argument-19` to `24`.
4. `tactic-unfalsifiable` is not in the skill's tactic table. The group's training plan names it among the flaws, so it has a piece, placed by the plan's order.
5. `tactic-rescues` has a group example only for the hot seat (an elder pressing the covenant arguments). For the professor's ploy it uses the method's general line.
6. The gospel is thin on sin, the resurrection and the call to believe. `gospel-6` uses Taleah Hall's story from the coach-only table of what moved former JWs; `gospel-10` uses Romans 10:9 from bank row 45. The call to repent has no quoted verse.
7. `gospel-4` says aloud that "of five former JWs whose accounts we have, four were moved by who Jesus is." That table is marked for the coach. Is saying it to trainees acceptable?
8. In the one-sentence argument, "almost every Witness" became "almost every Jehovah's Witness."
9. `their-answers-5` says "the Spirit's assurance," not "the Spirit's witness," to keep that word for the group.
10. **Two quotations in the SOP are not King James wording.** The pieces use the King James text; the SOP itself may need the fix.
    - "my blood of the covenant": the piece has "my blood of the new testament" (Matt. 26:28).
    - "Do this": the piece has "This do in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19).
11. Scripture copied from the response bank, not the SOP: Romans 8:14, Zechariah 8:23, Romans 10:9, and the "one fold" wording of John 10:16. Acts 8:35 is from the method material.
12. Framing lines that are the writer's: `know-them-3` ("Jesus' own words at his table will test it") and `foundation-9` ("a senior partner and a junior one").
13. Figures are rounded to the gist ("about twenty-four thousand of more than twenty million").
14. Left out of the answers because a tactic piece already teaches them: "more partakers means more are mistaken" and "the other sheep aren't in union with Jesus." Also left out: the Revelation 144,000 answer (it is in `tactic-staying-on-topic`).
15. `their-answers-11` (a former JW who no longer trusts the Bible) does not point to the approach for atheists, because a piece may not name other material.

## Jewish people (`jewish-people.md`)

1. The three exposing questions are in `argument-22` to `24`; their escapes are in `their-answers-3`, `8`, `9` and `11`.
2. Each flaw this group uses has its own tactic piece, though the skill's table groups the flaws into two rows. Fewer pieces may be wanted.
3. `tactic-rescues` covers both rescues as one. The material has no member line that shifts the burden onto the trainee; the hot-seat example comes from the training plan's pile-on of Hebrew points.
4. Wording the writer made:
    - `tactic-sibling-rivalry`: "Which of these two do you hold?" (the method's sample is "Which of these two is your real concern?").
    - `tactic-turnabout`: "What are you getting at?" is applied to the "despised and rejected" objection, which the material paraphrases.
    - `tactic-staying-on-topic`: the skill's parking line is changed from "once we've finished John 6" to "Isaiah fifty-three."
5. `argument-22` joins two sources: it uses the exact King James wording of verse eight from the exposing question where the short question list paraphrases it.
6. The training plan's first check asks about the Targum, the Talmud reading and the "forbidden chapter." The Targum and Talmud are taught in `know-them-5` and `argument-14`; the "forbidden chapter" is in `foundation-10` and `their-answers-15`.
7. Thin material: `background-3` (the branches of Judaism), `foundation-6` ("own it without excuse") and `their-answers-7` (the sacrifices as substitutes, and the ram in Isaac's place) are very short.
8. Small additions: "the Christian writer" before Origen (`know-them-6`); "the Day of Atonement" for Yom Kippur (`background-6`); "So the sins he suffers for are not his own" (`argument-6`), added to reach two sentences.
9. `gospel-5` (repentance) and `gospel-6` (Isaiah nine, verse six, and Jeremiah twenty-three, verse six) come from the group's recorded answers, not the SOP. The debate and street answers in that list (the Aish and Boteach objections, for example) are not taught as pieces.

## Atheists (`atheism.md`)

1. The escapes to the flaw questions open the answers (`their-answers-1` to `17`), ahead of the usual answers.
2. `tactic-rhodes-scholar` rests on one recorded response (the student's "almost no analytic philosopher"), and the training plan doesn't drill it. Keep the piece or cut it.
3. `tactic-just-the-facts`: the material files "Why do you expect the future to be like the past?" and the "chaotic" reply under this flaw, which fits "a claim built on wrong facts" only loosely.
4. `their-answers-7` keeps the material's wording that "It always has been" "uses the past to vouch for the past." That may be a slip for "the future."
5. The fifth question, the bridge question, is left out of the method and taught only under Turn to Christ.
6. Verses the material cites but doesn't quote are not quoted: Genesis 8:22, Malachi 3:6, Leviticus 19 and Matthew 5:22. Check that "by him all things consist" and "He cannot deny himself" are King James word for word, and whether the capital "He" is wanted.
7. Quotations trimmed for speaking: the bridge question drops its "(Rom. 1:19–21)"; the quotation about Philip begins at "began."
8. `tactic-staying-on-topic`: the narration example ("I asked where logic comes from, and we've moved to Christian wars. Could we finish that first?") is built from the skill's pattern. It is not a recorded line.
9. "Witness" is avoided for support, so "before many witnesses" became "many saw him risen" and "conscience bears witness" became "conscience testifies to it."
10. Short pieces: `argument-29` to `31` and `their-answers-6` and `8` are one sentence each, where the skill asks for two or three. They could be merged.
