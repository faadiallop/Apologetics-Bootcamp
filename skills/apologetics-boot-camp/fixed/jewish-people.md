# Jewish people (The Suffering Servant)

Status: draft

Source: skill stamp 3ed82382

## opening-beginner

Before we begin, take a moment to pray.

In this training we ask questions instead of making speeches, so that the other person's own Scripture does the work. Christ silenced his opponents with questions, and Greg Koukl's book Tactics trains the same skill.

Here is the argument for Jewish people in one sentence: Isaiah's servant is innocent, dies for the sins of God's people and then lives and is exalted; he cannot be the sinful nation he dies for, Israel's early interpreters saw the Messiah here, and Yeshua, which is Jesus' Hebrew name, is the one who fits.

Why this approach? Many Jewish people expect a Christian to start with the Trinity or the New Testament, which they don't accept. This approach starts in their own Tanakh, the Hebrew Bible, with the passage that most plainly foretells a servant who suffers for others' sins and then lives, a passage their own early tradition read of the Messiah. He reads it and says who fits, so the text makes the case, as it did for the Ethiopian official in Acts eight.

Here is what will happen. First come short lessons, then practice conversations in which I play a Jewish person, with quick coaching after each. Most of the practice goes to the parts of the method you find hardest, taken in the order Koukl teaches them. Once the method is sure, we turn to the gospel and how to cross to it; then come real conversations, and at the end you teach someone else.

If you know little about Jewish people, would you like to begin with some background on who they are?

## background-1

say: There are about fifteen point eight million Jewish people worldwide, most of them in Israel and the United States. Jewishness is a people as well as a faith, so a secular Jew with no religious belief is fully Jewish.

check: Is a secular Jew with no religious belief still Jewish?

## tactic-gather

say: Gathering means finding out what he believes before you answer it, so you neither misunderstand nor misrepresent him. Your chance comes when he uses a loaded word or states his view loosely, and the question is, "What do you mean by that?" With a Jewish person, ask whether he has read Isaiah fifty-three, whom he thinks it describes, and who taught him that.

check: When does your chance to gather come?

expect: When he uses a loaded word or states his view loosely; ask what he means.

## tactic-burden-of-proof

say: The burden of proof stays on whoever makes the claim, so he gives reasons, not only opinions. Your chance comes whenever he makes a claim, and the question is, "How did you come to that conclusion?" If he says the servant is Israel, ask, "What in the text shows the servant is Israel here?"

check: Who carries the burden of proof?

expect: Whoever makes the claim; ask how he came to that conclusion.

## tactic-rescues

say: Two rescues keep you steady: if he shifts the burden onto you, remember that if you have made no claim, you owe no proof, and say, "I've only asked questions so far. Your view is the one on the table." If he outclasses you, as when he piles on Hebrew points about lamo, seed and the righteous remnant, switch from persuading to fact-finding: ask him to slow down and explain, take notes, and close with, "Let me think about it."

check: What do you do when he piles on Hebrew points you can't answer?

expect: Switch to fact-finding: ask him to slow down and explain, take notes, and say "Let me think about it." (If he shifts the burden: no claim, no proof owed.)

## tactic-staying-on-topic

say: When he raises a side issue or slides off the question, bring the talk back to the one question by the way that fits: let a passing jab pass, park a real question or hurt, or narrate when he keeps drifting. To park, honor it in one sentence, then ask, "Can we come back to that once we've finished Isaiah fifty-three?" If he raises Christian persecution, or why Jesus didn't bring peace, honor it, park it, and return to Isaiah fifty-three.

check: What are the three ways back to the one question?

expect: Let a passing jab pass, park a real question or hurt, or narrate when he keeps drifting.

## tactic-steamroller

say: A steamroller interrupts and piles on, and each new interruption calls for the next step: stop him, shame him, then leave him. At the first, say, "I'm not quite finished," and at the next ask gently, "Do you want an answer, or an audience?" At the one after, leave with no jab and the door open, with an offer to read Isaiah fifty-three together another time.

check: What are the three steps, one for each interruption?

expect: Stop him, shame him gently, leave him (no jab, door open).

## tactic-lead

say: Leading means asking questions that make the point, so he sees it himself instead of hearing you assert it. Your chance comes once he has stated his view and his reasons, and each question walks him one step toward the point, like, "In fifty-two, verses four to six, whom does God call 'my people'?" After the question that exposes the flaw, wait in silence.

check: When does your chance to lead with questions come?

expect: Once he has stated his view and his reasons.

## tactic-turnabout

say: When he leads you with questions toward a point he hasn't stated, or makes a claim in the form of a question, don't answer the trap; ask him to state his point, then ask his reasons for it. For example, if he asks how the servant can be despised and rejected when great crowds followed Jesus, ask, "What are you getting at?"

check: What do you do when he makes a claim in the form of a question?

expect: Don't answer the trap; ask him to state his point, then ask his reasons.

## tactic-sibling-rivalry

say: Sibling Rivalry is two of his own claims that cancel each other, and your chance comes when he holds both. The question sets them side by side: "Which of these two do you hold?" If he says no one can die for another's sin, yet reads the passage as innocent Israel suffering for the nations' sins, ask which he holds.

check: What is Sibling Rivalry?

expect: Two of his own claims that cancel each other; set them side by side with a question.

## tactic-taking-the-roof-off

say: Taking the Roof Off follows a view consistently until it ends in absurdity, and your chance comes when he holds such a view. The question is, "If that's true, wouldn't it also follow that…?" If Israel's sufferings atoned for the nations, then the nations that persecuted Jews were forgiven by that persecution; ask gently whether he believes that.

check: Where does the view that Israel's sufferings atoned for the nations lead?

expect: The nations that persecuted Jews were forgiven by that persecution; ask gently whether he believes it.

## tactic-rhodes-scholar

say: Rhodes Scholar catches an appeal to experts without their reasons, and your chance comes when he leans on authorities alone. The question is, "What reasons does that scholar give?" If he says, "The prevailing rabbinic interpretation is that the servant is Israel," ask, "What did the Targum and the Talmud say?"

check: What question answers an appeal to experts without their reasons?

expect: What reasons does that scholar give? (Here: what did the Targum and the Talmud say?)

## tactic-just-the-facts

say: Just the Facts catches a claim built on wrong facts, and your chance comes when he rests on one; the question is, "What exactly is the claim? Is it accurate?" If he says lamo in Isaiah fifty-three, verse eight, must be plural, Isaiah forty-four, verse fifteen, uses lamo of a single idol.

check: Where does Isaiah use lamo of a single thing?

expect: Isaiah 44:15, of a single idol.
