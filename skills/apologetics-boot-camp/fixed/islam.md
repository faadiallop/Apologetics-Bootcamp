# Muslims (The Islamic Dilemma)

Status: draft

Source: skill stamp 8afceae0

## opening-beginner

Before we begin, take a moment to pray.

Here is the method. We ask questions instead of making speeches, so the other person's own scripture does the work. Christ silenced his opponents with a question about David's son, and Greg Koukl's book Tactics trains the same skill.

The argument is called the Islamic Dilemma. The Qur'an says the Torah and Gospel in Christian and Jewish hands came from God and bound them, yet it denies what those books teach; so either the Qur'an contradicts itself, or a Muslim must deny what the Qur'an says.

Why this approach? Talks with Muslims often stall on side questions: whether the Bible is reliable, which reports of Muhammad's words are sound, or the Trinity. This approach uses only the Qur'an, which every Muslim accepts, and two things a Muslim already believes: that the Qur'an confirms the Torah and Gospel, and that Jesus was not crucified. Your questions set those beliefs side by side, so you have nothing of your own to defend. And the Qur'an itself sends the doubter to the Gospel, where the conversation ends.

Here is what will happen. First come short lessons, each with one question to check it. Then come practice conversations in which I play a Muslim, with quick coaching after each. Most of the practice goes to the parts of the method you find hardest, taken in the order Koukl teaches them. Once the method is sure, you'll learn the gospel and how to cross to it. Then come real conversations, and at the end you'll teach someone else.

Would you like to begin with some background on who Muslims are?

## background-1

say: Muslims number nearly two billion people. Most are Sunni and a minority are Shia, but both hold the same Qur'an, which is why this approach uses the Qur'an alone.

check: Why does this approach use only the Qur'an?

expect: Sunni and Shia alike hold the same Qur'an, so it is common ground with every Muslim.

## background-2

say: Muhammad was born in Mecca about the year 570 and died in 632. Muslims hold him to be the last prophet, who received the Qur'an through the angel Gabriel.

check: What do Muslims believe about Muhammad?

expect: He is the last prophet, and he received the Qur'an through the angel Gabriel. Dates are not required.

## background-3

check: Which book does Islam say was given through Jesus?

expect: The Injil. Naming the Tawrat through Moses or the Zabur through David as well is fine but not required.

## foundation-1

say: Islam honors Jesus as the Messiah but denies the Father and the Son, and the Qur'an makes both denials. Scripture names that denial plainly, in First John two, verses twenty-two and twenty-three: "He is antichrist, that denieth the Father and the Son. Whosoever denieth the Son, the same hath not the Father."

check: What does First John say of the one who denies the Son?

expect: He has not the Father; the one who denies the Father and the Son is antichrist. The gist, not the exact words.

## foundation-2

say: God does not contradict himself, so any message that claims to come from him is judged by what he has already said. Isaiah eight, verse twenty: "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them." Even the Bereans tested Paul's preaching against the Scriptures.

check: By what is a message that claims to come from God judged?

expect: By what God has already said, the earlier word; if it does not agree with it, there is no light in it.

## foundation-3

say: Paul applies the same test even to an angel, in Galatians one, verse eight: "Though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed." That bears on Islam, whose message claims to have come through an angel.

check: Why does that verse in Galatians bear on Islam?

expect: Islam's message claims to have come through an angel, and any other gospel, even from an angel, is accursed.

## foundation-4

say: This is why the Islamic Dilemma works: it is not neutral logic, but God's own test of a prophet. And the Qur'an has agreed to be tested this way, for it claims to confirm the earlier books, and it says a book not from God would contain much contradiction.

check: How has the Qur'an agreed to be tested?

expect: It claims to confirm the earlier books, and it says a book not from God would contain much contradiction. Either point, rightly stated, with the sense that this is God's test, not neutral logic.

check: Where does that verse send the one who is in doubt?

## argument-9

say: Fourth, it requires belief in them. Muslims must believe in what was given to Moses and to Jesus.

## argument-10

say: Fifth, it says the books were written and present among Jews and Christians. It calls Muhammad one "whom they find written down with them in the Tawrat and the Injeel."

check: What does the Qur'an say about where Muhammad is found written?

expect: In the Tawrat and the Injil that were with them, written down; so the books were present in their hands.

## argument-11

say: The books they held then were the Torah and the four Gospels, and manuscripts from before Muhammad show what those books said. Among them are the Dead Sea Scrolls, Codex Sinaiticus from the fourth century, and the Ethiopian Garima Gospels, carbon-dated between 330 and 650.

check: How do we know what the Torah and Gospels said before Muhammad?

expect: From manuscripts made before him, such as the Dead Sea Scrolls, Codex Sinaiticus or the Garima Gospels. Naming one is enough.

## argument-12

say: The second premise is that the Qur'an contradicts those same books. The clearest case is the cross: all four Gospels say Jesus was crucified and died, but the Qur'an says, "they did not kill him nor did they crucify him."

check: What does the second premise say, and what is its clearest case?

expect: The Qur'an contradicts the same books; the Gospels say Jesus was crucified and died, the Qur'an says he was not.

## argument-13

check: Besides the cross, what other teaching about Jesus does the Qur'an deny?

expect: That he is the Son of God, which the Gospels teach.

## argument-14

say: The Qur'an denies that Jesus is God. But John's Gospel says, "the Word was God," and Thomas calls Jesus "My Lord and my God."

check: What does Thomas call Jesus?

expect: My Lord and my God.

## argument-15

say: The Qur'an denies that God is Father to his people, saying that their punishment proves he is not their father. But the Torah speaks of God as his people's father, in Deuteronomy thirty-two, and Jesus taught us to pray to our Father.

check: What reason does the Qur'an give that God is not his people's father?

## argument-16

say: The Qur'an claims the earlier books foretell Muhammad, but they foretell no illiterate or Gentile prophet. The prophet like Moses comes "of thy brethren," and the Helper Jesus promised is the Holy Spirit.

check: Who is the Helper that Jesus promised?

## argument-17

## argument-18

say: The conclusion is a dilemma. If the Qur'an affirms these books and also contradicts them, it affirms what it denies, and by its own test it is not from God. If a Muslim denies that the Qur'an affirms these books, he denies what the Qur'an plainly says.

check: What are the two sides of the dilemma?

expect: Either the Qur'an affirms what it denies and by its own test is not from God, or the Muslim denies what the Qur'an plainly says.

## argument-19

say: Frost presents this as an argument to the best explanation. The most likely account, he says, is that the Qur'an's author knew these books through oral tradition, not the written text, and erred.

check: What does Frost take to be the most likely explanation?

expect: The Qur'an's author knew the books through oral tradition, not the written text, and erred.

## argument-20

say: Use only the Qur'an. Every sect accepts it, and it is the earliest Islamic source, while hadith and commentary differ by sect and open side doors.

check: Why not use hadith?

expect: Hadith and commentary differ by sect and open side doors; the Qur'an is accepted by every sect and is the earliest source.

## argument-21

say: Keep it narrow: win agreement on the first premise before the second, and choose clear examples. The crucifixion is plain in all four Gospels and needs no theology to explain, and one clear contradiction, well established, does more than ten rushed ones.

check: Why is the crucifixion the example to use?

expect: It is plain in all four Gospels and needs no theology to explain; one clear contradiction does more than many rushed ones.

## argument-22

say: Avoid hadith debates, arguing about Bible manuscripts beyond their date, the Trinity before the dilemma lands, saying "the Bible" when you mean Tawrat or Injil, and any contempt. Until the dilemma lands, the question is what the Qur'an affirms.

check: Name two things to avoid in the conversation.

expect: Any two of: hadith debates; arguing about manuscripts beyond their date; the Trinity before the dilemma lands; saying "the Bible" for Tawrat or Injil; contempt.

## argument-23

say: The questions run in this order. First ask, "When chapter five, verse sixty-eight tells the Jews and Christians of Muhammad's day to keep up the Tawrat and the Injil, which books did they have?" Then, "What do the Gospels say happened to Jesus? What does the Qur'an say?" and then, "Can a book confirm another book and deny its central event?"

check: What is the first question you ask?

expect: Which books the Jews and Christians of Muhammad's day had when the Qur'an told them to keep up the Tawrat and the Injil. The substance, in any wording.

## argument-24

say: Then ask, "Were the Torah and Gospel corrupted before Muhammad, after him, or never?" If before, the Qur'an told them to keep corrupted books; if after, the copies made before him read as ours do; if never, the cross stands. Ask it, and then wait in silence.

check: If he says the books were corrupted after Muhammad, what does that answer cost him?

expect: The copies made before Muhammad read as ours do, so the books the Qur'an confirmed teach the cross.

## tactic-gather

say: The first step is to gather: ask, "What do you mean by that?" until you understand his view, then feed it back, "Let me see if I understand you." Its moment comes when he uses a loaded word or states his view loosely. When a Muslim says the original Injil was lost, ask, "What is the Injil? Who gave it, and to whom?"

check: When a Muslim states his view loosely, what do you do?

expect: Ask what he means, with questions such as "What do you mean by that?", before answering; then feed his view back to him.

## tactic-burden-of-proof

say: The second step keeps the burden of proof on whoever makes the claim: ask, "How did you come to that conclusion?" so he gives reasons, not only opinions. Its moment comes whenever he makes a main claim. When he says the Injil was changed, ask, "How do you know the Injil was changed? Where does the Qur'an say the text was corrupted?"

check: When he claims the Injil was changed, what do you ask him?

expect: For his reasons: how he knows it was changed, or where the Qur'an says the text was corrupted. Not arguing against the claim himself, and not a soft "Are you sure?"

## tactic-rescues-professors-ploy

say: The professor's ploy is a rescue for when he shifts the burden onto you, asking you to prove what you never claimed. If you have made no claim, you owe no proof: "I've only asked questions so far. Your view is the one on the table." When he asks, "If the Qur'an were from Satan, why would it send you to the Bible?", say you haven't claimed a source; you've shown that it affirms what it denies.

check: What do you say when he asks you to prove something you never claimed?

expect: That he has made no such claim and owes no proof: he has only asked questions, and the Muslim's view is the one on the table.

## tactic-rescues-hot-seat

say: The hot seat is a rescue for when he outclasses you. Switch from persuading to fact-finding: ask him to slow down and explain his view and his reasons, take notes, and close with "Let me think about it." When a learned Muslim cites the Arabic word for guardian, the kinds of distortion and the history of the canon, ask him to explain, and research it later.

check: When a Muslim knows far more than you, what do you do?

expect: Switch to fact-finding: ask him to explain his view and reasons, take notes, and say "Let me think about it"; no bluffing and no giving up.

## tactic-staying-on-topic

say: When he raises a side issue or slides off the question, take the way back that fits: let a passing jab pass, park a real question or a hurt in one sentence, or narrate when he keeps drifting, naming the dropped question. Then return to the question by name, with a question that ties back to it. If he moves to blasphemy law, say, "We've moved from the Injil to blasphemy law. Can we go back?"

check: When a side issue comes up, what three ways back can you take?

expect: Let it pass, park it, or narrate; each fitted to the moment, and each returning to the one question by name.

## tactic-steamroller

say: A steamroller interrupts and piles on challenges, as a fast street preacher may with the end of Mark and two Injils. At his first interruption, stop him: "Can I finish this one point?" At the second, shame him gently, "I've heard all your points without cutting in. Is it fair to ask the same?", and at the third, leave with no jab, the door open and a Gospel offered.

check: What do you do at his third interruption?

expect: Leave: no jab, no motive named, the door left open and a Gospel offered.

## tactic-lead

say: The third step leads with questions once he has given his view and his reasons, each question walking him a step toward the point so the flaw shows without your stating it. Ask, for example, "In chapter five, verse forty-seven, what were the Christians of Muhammad's day told to judge by?" and then, "Would Allah command them to judge by a corrupted book?", until he states both premises in his own words.

check: When does this third step begin?

expect: Once he has stated his view and his reasons; then questions, not statements, lead him toward the point.

## tactic-turnabout

say: The turnabout is for when he leads you with questions toward a point he hasn't stated, or makes a claim in the form of a question. Don't answer the trap; ask him to state his point, "What are you getting at?", and then ask his reasons for it. If a learned Muslim presses, "Is that view incoherent, yes or no?", ask first, "Are you saying…?"

check: When he leads you with a question toward a point he hasn't stated, what do you do?

expect: Not answer the trap; ask him to state his point, for example "What are you getting at?", then ask his reasons.

## tactic-suicide

say: Suicide is a claim that fails its own standard. Its moment comes once he has granted both premises, for his own book says a book not from God would contain much contradiction. Ask, "Can a book confirm another book and deny its central event?", and let his book's own test do the rest.

check: Which standard of his own does the Qur'an fail here?

expect: Its own test: a book not from God would contain much contradiction, and the Qur'an affirms what it denies.

## tactic-sibling-rivalry

say: Sibling rivalry is two of his own claims that cancel each other. Its moment comes when he holds both that the Qur'an confirms the Injil and that Jesus was not crucified. Ask, "Chapter five, verse forty-seven tells the Christians of Muhammad's day to judge by the Injil. Was the Injil they had then reliable, or corrupted?"

check: Which two of his claims cancel each other?

expect: That the Qur'an confirms the Injil, and that Jesus was not crucified, which the Injil records.

## tactic-infanticide

say: Infanticide is a claim that depends on the very thing it denies. Its moment comes when he calls the Gospel corrupt yet uses it to find Muhammad, who his book says is "written down with them in the Tawrat and the Injeel." Ask, "How can you use that source if it's unreliable?"

check: What does his claim depend on that it also denies?

expect: The Gospel: he calls it corrupt, yet relies on it to show that Muhammad was foretold.

## tactic-taking-the-roof-off

say: Taking the roof off follows a view consistently until it ends in absurdity. Its moment comes when he says it only appeared that Jesus was crucified. Ask, "If that's true, wouldn't it also follow that no eyewitness can be trusted, including those who passed on the Qur'an?"

check: Where does the claim that the crucifixion only appeared to happen lead?

expect: If God deceived the eyewitnesses, no testimony can be trusted, including the testimony that passed on the Qur'an.

## tactic-rhodes-scholar

say: Rhodes scholar is an appeal to experts without their reasons. Its moment comes when he says Christian scholars admit the Bible was corrupted. Ask, "Which scholar, and what reasons?"

check: When he says scholars agree with him, what do you ask?

expect: Which scholar, and what reasons that scholar gives.

## tactic-just-the-facts

say: Just the facts catches a claim built on wrong facts: ask first whether it looks unlikely on its face, then check it. Its moment comes when he says the Gospels Christians held before Muhammad did not tell of the cross. Ask, "Did the Gospels Christians held before Muhammad say Jesus was crucified and died?" Codex Sinaiticus, from the fourth century, shows they did.

check: What fact answers the claim that the Gospels before Muhammad did not tell of the cross?

expect: Manuscripts from before Muhammad, such as Codex Sinaiticus, tell of the crucifixion and death.

## their-answers-1

say: Every common escape either fails against the Qur'an's own words or makes the dilemma worse. Muslim scholars have no agreed answer, which is itself telling.

check: What happens to each common escape from the dilemma?

expect: It fails against the Qur'an's own words or makes the dilemma worse.

## their-answers-2

say: Some say the Qur'an's Injil is a different book, given to Jesus, not the four Gospels. But Injil comes from the word the church used for the four Gospels, and the Qur'an retells stories found only in them and names the Injil beside one. It says the Injil was written down "with them," and no other Gospel has ever been found.

check: How do you show that the Qur'an's Injil is the Gospels the church held?

expect: Any of: the name comes from the church's word for the Gospels; the Qur'an retells stories found only in them; it says the Injil was written down with them; no other Injil has been found.

## their-answers-3

say: Some say the Qur'an means the original books, not what Jews and Christians had then. But it speaks of what they have now, "That which is with you," and says "They have the Tawrat." Its Jesus confirmed the Torah of his own day, and manuscripts show what that Torah said.

check: What wording in the Qur'an answers the claim that it means only the original books?

expect: It speaks of what is "with you" and says "they have the Tawrat": the books in their hands then.

## their-answers-4

say: Some say the Qur'an confirms only the true parts. But it never says which parts, it condemns believing part of scripture and rejecting the rest, and the parts it denies, the cross and the Son, are the center of the Gospel. Ask how he knows which parts are true without first assuming the Qur'an is right.

check: What do you ask a Muslim who says the Qur'an confirms only the true parts?
