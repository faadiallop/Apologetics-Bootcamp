# Training Plans

Each plan walks a trainee through one SOP's argument and Koukl's tactics, stage by stage, with agents playing the other side and coaching.

## How training works

Every plan runs the same loop: learn the material, drill one skill at a time against an agent, then face full conversations that grow harder. The stages follow the outline of the SOP and, for the method, the chapter order of Koukl's *Tactics* (2019 edition), given beside each skill; that order never changes, except that the steamroller (ch. 14) is drilled right after the rescues, since it too keeps control of the conversation. The time goes to what the trainee finds hardest. One new to the group is taught each part before he is checked on it, and a stage he passes at once takes little time; one who isn't starts with a full conversation and drills only the tactics he misses, in stage order. He moves on only when the coach scores a pass. The gospel and the bridge to Christ have their own stage, after the method, so that he learns one thing at a time: "first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear" (Mark 4:28).

**Roles**

| Role | Who | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trainee | A person or an agent in training | Answers using the SOP and Methodology: Questioning Them to Death |
| Opponent | An agent | Plays a persona from Response Bank (role-play), one response at a time |
| Coach | An agent | Sets each drill, watches, scores, and explains each score from the transcript |

**A round**

1. The coach names the stage, the persona and the goal.
2. The opponent opens with a response from the bank.
3. The trainee and opponent talk only as many turns as it takes to pass the round, about the number estimated for the stage (four for Gather, about fifteen for a full conversation; the boot camp skill gives the rest). When its goal is met or its turns run out, in every round but Stage 10 and the full conversations, the opponent shows in character that he is about to leave ("Anyway, I should get going"); this is the trainee's only cue, and the coach announces nothing. From there the trainee has up to two closing turns to turn to Christ in a sentence, in his own words; a question about Christ, or an invitation to read or hear of him, counts. Once he does, the opponent answers in a sentence and the round closes. In the steamroller stage the trainee is the one who leaves, so his own parting words are his closing turn. The closing turns are not among the round's turns and are never scored, and a pass never waits on them, except that a jab or mockery costs him on character wherever it comes (Col. 4:6). Each time he lets both pass, or takes them without pointing to Christ by name or title, the coach reminds him that the parting was the moment, with a sentence built from the round's own words if the round supplies one; each group's plan gives two samples (Matt. 10:7; Acts 8:35; Acts 17:18; 1 Cor. 2:2).
4. The coach scores the round on the rubric below, quoting the turns that earned or lost each point, and counts the chances and hits for each tactic that came up.
5. The trainee writes one thing to do differently, and adds any new challenge to the Response Bank.

**Drill and prayer.** Training is drill for a real war (2 Tim. 2:3–4): "the more you sweat in training, the less you bleed in battle." The coach reminds human trainees to pray at the start of each session (Eph. 6:18) but does not teach them how, and the coach marks *character* down for any round that treats the person as the enemy instead of the captive (Eph. 6:12).

**Rubric** (Koukl's three skills of an ambassador; score each 0–2)

| Skill | 2 = pass | 0 = fail sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Knowledge** | States the premises and what their supporting passages say, correctly (references not required) | Misstates what a passage says, or gets a fact badly wrong; argues beyond the evidence |
| **Wisdom** | Asks before telling; keeps the burden on whoever made the claim; names and exposes the flaw with a question | Lectures; accepts a burden for a claim not made; chases a side issue |
| **Character** | Calm, gracious, fair to their view, and shows care for the person, not restraint alone, in any one of these ways: asks about him, hears him out, or answers his hurt kindly (1 Cor. 13:2; Mark 6:34; Phil. 2:4); from Stage 10 on, ends with an invitation or a Gospel to read | Gets angry, mocks, or interrupts; wins the point and loses the person |

**Passing a stage.** Meet the stage's "Pass when" line and score 5 or more out of 6, with no 0 in any skill, in two rounds in a row; Stage 7, Lead (Columbo step 3), takes three rounds in a row, and Stage 9, Find the flaw, takes three passing sets in a row, each of three rounds; a set passes on two of its three rounds, and a failed set starts the count of three again.

**Ground rule for every round.** The goal is a stone in the shoe, not a knockout (1 Cor. 3:6–7). A round where the trainee "wins" but scores 0 on character fails.
