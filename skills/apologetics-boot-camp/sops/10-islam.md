# Islam

The Qur'an claims to confirm the Torah and Gospel that Jews and Christians held in Muhammad's day, yet it denies what they teach. This SOP presses that contradiction, then turns the conversation to the Gospel the Qur'an itself points to.

## Short version

**The argument in one sentence.** The Qur'an says the Torah and Gospel in Christian and Jewish hands came from God and bound them, yet it denies what those books teach; so either the Qur'an contradicts itself, or a Muslim must deny what the Qur'an says.

**Before you go: pray.** Pray for the person by name, that God would open his eyes and grant repentance, and for clear and bold words for yourself (Eph. 6:18–20; 2 Tim. 2:25–26; Col. 4:3–4).

**Ask, in order.** Wait in silence after the question that exposes the flaw.

1. "When Q 5:68 tells the Jews and Christians of Muhammad's day to keep up the Tawrat and the Injil, which books did they have?"
2. "What do the Gospels say happened to Jesus? What does the Qur'an say?" (Mark 15:24; Q 4:157)
3. "Can a book confirm another book and deny its central event?"
4. **Sibling Rivalry:** "Were the Torah and Gospel corrupted before Muhammad, after him, or never?" *Before:* the Qur'an told them to keep corrupted books. *After:* the copies made before him read as ours do. *Never:* the cross stands.
5. "Your book tells the doubter to ask those who read the Book before (Q 10:94). Will you read Luke with me?"

**Avoid:** hadith debates; debating Bible manuscripts (only their date matters here); the Trinity before the dilemma lands; saying "the Bible" when you mean Tawrat or Injil; contempt. The long version answers every objection.

## Long version

Learn this before the conversation. It explains every step of the short version and shows its support.

**Have open:** Qur'an 2:41; 3:3; 4:157; 5:43–47; 5:68; 7:157; 9:30; 10:94. Bible: Matt. 3:17; Mark 2:5–12; John 1:1, 14; John 20:28–31.

### A. Foundation

Islam honors Jesus as Messiah but denies the Father and the Son, and Scripture names that denial plainly: "This is the antichrist, he who denies the Father and the Son. No one who denies the Son has the Father" (1 John 2:22–23, ESV). The Qur'an makes both denials (Q 5:18; 9:30; 4:171).

**A later word is tested by the earlier.** God does not contradict himself (Num. 23:19; Titus 1:2; Ps. 119:160). So any message that claims to come from him is judged by what he has already said:

- "To the teaching and to the testimony! If they will not speak according to this word, it is because they have no dawn" (Isa. 8:20, ESV).
- "Even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed" (Gal. 1:8, ESV). Islam's message claims to have come through an angel.
- The Bereans tested even Paul's preaching against the Scriptures (Acts 17:11).

This is why the Islamic Dilemma works. It is not neutral logic. It is God's own test of a prophet, and the Qur'an has agreed to be tested this way. It claims to confirm the earlier books (Q 3:3) and says a book not from God would contain much contradiction (Q 4:82).

**How we carry ourselves.** Most Muslims we meet are devout and sincere, with a zeal for God that lacks knowledge, as Paul said of Israel (Rom. 10:1–2).

- Default to gentleness and respect (1 Pet. 3:15; 2 Tim. 2:24–25; Col. 4:6).
- Show mercy to those who doubt (Jude 22–23).
- Sharper words belong only to those who lead others astray, such as public polemicists. Even then they must be true, brief and for the hearers' good (Matt. 23:13; Titus 1:10–13).

### B. Know them

A Muslim holds the Qur'an to be Allah's own word, and most today believe the Bible was corrupted. The Qur'an itself says otherwise, and that gap is where the method works.

**Background for beginners.** What a newcomer should know about Muslims, as it bears on this approach:

- **Who they are.** Nearly two billion people. Most are Sunni, a minority Shia; both hold the same Qur'an, which is why this approach uses the Qur'an alone.
- **Muhammad.** Born in Mecca about 570, died 632. Muslims hold him the last prophet, who received the Qur'an through the angel Gabriel.
- **The prophets and their books.** Islam honors earlier prophets, Abraham, Moses, David and Jesus among them, and names books given through them: the Tawrat (Moses), the Zabur (David) and the Injil (Jesus). This is the door the approach walks through.
- **Jesus (Isa).** Born of a virgin, the Messiah, a worker of miracles, but not God's Son and not crucified. Many Muslims expect him to return.
- **Practice.** The Five Pillars: the confession of faith, prayer five times a day, giving to the poor, the fast of Ramadan, and the pilgrimage to Mecca.
- **Courtesy.** Treat the Qur'an with respect (never set it on the floor), accept hospitality, and speak about what the books say, not about Muslims as people.
- **Dawah.** Many Muslims are trained to invite others to Islam, and street preachers often have practiced answers.

**Final authority.** The Qur'an, believed to be sent down to Muhammad through the angel Gabriel from about 610 to 632. Every Muslim sect accepts it. Hadith (reports of Muhammad's words and deeds), tafsir (commentary) and sira (biography) also carry weight, but which ones count differs between Sunni and Shia.

**View of the earlier books.** The Qur'an names the Torah, Psalms and Gospel as revelations from Allah. It requires belief in Allah's books and messengers (Q 2:136; 2:285; 4:136). Most Muslims today hold that the Bible was corrupted (*tahrif*). Frost traces that hardened view to Ibn Hazm (d. 1064); earlier Muslim scholars often used the Bible as a source.

**Jesus (Isa).** Son of Mary, born of a virgin, the Messiah, "a Word from Allah," a prophet who worked miracles "with Allah's permission" (Q 3:45–49). He is not the Son of God and not God (Q 4:171; 5:116), and he was not crucified (Q 4:157). Most Muslims believe someone else was made to look like him.

**God (Allah).** One, with no son and no partner. He is not a father to anyone (Q 5:18; 6:101; 9:30). His love is given to the righteous and withheld from unbelievers (Q 3:32).

### C. The method: the Islamic Dilemma

The argument has two premises and one conclusion. Get agreement on each premise before moving to the next.

**Why this approach.** Talks with Muslims often stall on side questions: whether the Bible is reliable, which hadith are sound, or the Trinity. This approach uses only the Qur'an, which every Muslim accepts, and two things a Muslim already believes: that the Qur'an confirms the Torah and Gospel, and that Jesus was not crucified. The questions set those beliefs side by side, so the Christian has nothing of his own to defend (Prov. 26:5). And the Qur'an itself sends the doubter to the Gospel (Q 10:94), where the conversation ends.

#### Premise 1: The Qur'an affirms the Torah and Gospel that Jews and Christians held in Muhammad's day

The Qur'an affirms these books in five independent ways. Martin Whittingham counts 119 verses that speak well of the earlier scripture against 21 that speak ill of it (cited in Frost).

1. **It confirms them.** The word *musaddiq* ("confirming") is used of them in 18 verses. Some speak of what is "with you" now (Q 2:41; 2:91; 4:47), others more broadly (Q 3:3; 5:46).
2. **It holds people accountable to them.** The Jews "have the Tawrat wherein is Allah's judgment" (Q 5:43). "Let the followers of the Injeel judge by what Allah has revealed in it" (Q 5:47). "You follow no good till you keep up the Tawrat and the Injeel" (Q 5:68).
3. **It sends people to them.** "If you are in doubt as to what We have revealed to you, ask those who read the Book before you" (Q 10:94; also 16:43; 21:7).
4. **It requires belief in them.** Muslims must believe in what was given to Moses and Jesus (Q 2:136; 4:136).
5. **It says they were written and present.** Muhammad is one "whom they find written down with them in the Tawrat and the Injeel" (Q 7:157).

Those books were the Torah and the four Gospels. Manuscripts from before Muhammad show their content: the Dead Sea Scrolls (4th century BC to 2nd century AD), Codex Sinaiticus (4th century) and the Ethiopian Garima Gospels (carbon-dated 330–650).

#### Premise 2: The Qur'an contradicts those same books

| Teaching | Qur'an | Torah and Gospel (ESV) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jesus was crucified | Denied: "they did not kill him nor did they crucify him" (4:157) | Matt. 27:35; Mark 15:24; Luke 23:33; John 19:18 |
| Jesus is the Son of God | Denied (9:30; 4:171; 19:88–92) | Matt. 3:17; Mark 14:61–62; John 1:14 |
| Jesus is God | Denied (5:72; 5:116) | John 1:1; John 20:28 |
| God is Father to his people | Denied: punishment proves he is not their father (5:18) | Deut. 8:5; 32:6; Matt. 6:9 |
| Muhammad is foretold | Claimed (7:157) | No illiterate or Gentile prophet is foretold. The prophet like Moses comes "from your brothers" (Deut. 18:15); the promised Helper is the Holy Spirit (John 14:26) |
| The Gospel commands holy war | Claimed (9:111) | "All who take the sword will perish by the sword" (Matt. 26:52); "My kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36) |
| Marriage continues in paradise | Taught (2:25; 36:55–56) | "In the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage" (Matt. 22:30) |

#### Conclusion: the dilemma

- **Incoherence.** If the Qur'an affirms these books and also contradicts them, it affirms what it denies. By its own test it is not from God (Q 4:82).
- **Leaving the Qur'an.** If a Muslim denies that the Qur'an affirms these books, he denies what the Qur'an plainly says.

Frost presents this as an argument to the best explanation: the most likely account is that the Qur'an's author knew these books through oral tradition, not the written text, and erred.

#### Ground rules

- **Use only the Qur'an.** Every sect accepts it, and it is the earliest Islamic source. Hadith and commentary differ by sect and open side doors.
- **Use their terms.** Say Tawrat and Injil, so the talk stays on what the Qur'an means.
- **Choose clear examples.** The crucifixion is plain in all four Gospels and needs no theology to explain.
- **One premise at a time.** Don't argue too broadly. Win agreement on Premise 1 before Premise 2.
- **Ask, don't only tell.** "What books did the Christians of Muhammad's day have when Q 5:47 told them to judge by the Injil?"

#### Question plan

Run the dilemma as questions, following the three steps of Methodology: Questioning Them to Death. The Muslim already holds both premises; the questions set them side by side.

1. **Gather.** "What is the Injil? Who gave it, and to whom?" "When Q 7:157 says the Injil is 'written down with them,' who had it?"
2. **Burden of proof.** "How do you know the Injil was changed? Where does the Qur'an say the text was corrupted?" "Has anyone ever found a different Injil?"
3. **Lead.**
   - "In Q 5:47, what were the Christians of Muhammad's day told to judge by?"
   - "Would Allah command them to judge by a corrupted book?"
   - "What do all four Gospels say happened to Jesus?"
   - "Can a book confirm another book and deny its central event?"
   - "Your book tells the doubter to ask those who read the Book before him (Q 10:94). Would you read one Gospel with me?"

If he says the Qur'an only confirms the true parts, ask how he knows which parts are true without assuming the Qur'an is right. That is the very question at issue.

#### Exposing the flaws

Ask these last, after the Muslim has affirmed both premises. Every answer costs him. Then wait in silence.

| Question | It sets against each other | Every answer costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sibling Rivalry.** **"Q 5:47 tells the Christians of Muhammad's day to judge by the Injil. Was the Injil they had then reliable, or corrupted?"** | Q 5:47 (judge by it) and Q 4:157 (no crucifixion) | *Reliable:* it records the crucifixion, so the Qur'an denies what it confirmed. *Corrupted:* Allah commanded judging by a corrupt book, and the Qur'an confirmed it. *"The lost original":* then the command could not be obeyed. |
| **Just the Facts.** **"Did the Gospels Christians held before Muhammad say Jesus was crucified?"** | The Qur'an's confirmation (Q 3:3; 5:46) and the manuscripts | *Yes:* the Qur'an confirmed books that teach the cross. *No:* Codex Sinaiticus (4th century) says otherwise. |
| **Burden of proof (Columbo, step 2).** **"If the Qur'an confirms only the true parts, how do you know which parts are true without first assuming the Qur'an is right?"** | General confirmation and the question at issue | Any answer either names no test or assumes what is being tested. |
| **Sibling Rivalry.** **"Q 10:94 sends Muhammad, if in doubt, to those who read the Book before him. Would Allah send his prophet to a corrupted book for reassurance?"** | Q 10:94 and the corruption claim | *Yes:* Allah sends his prophet to falsehood. *No:* the Book they read was sound. |

**Pitfalls**

- Getting drawn into hadith or commentary, which vary by sect.
- Debating the whole biblical canon. The Torah and the four Gospels are enough.
- Defending the Trinity before the dilemma has landed. The question is what the Qur'an affirms.
- Piling up points. One clear contradiction, well established, does more than ten rushed ones.
- Treating a sincere friend like a public debater.

### A′. Turn to Christ

The Qur'an itself sends the Muslim to the Gospel, so take him there. The dilemma clears the ground; the Gospel is what we came to give.

1. **Use the door the Qur'an opens.** "Your book says to ask those who read the Book before you (Q 10:94) and to judge by the Injil (Q 5:47). Shall we read it together?"
2. **Start where he is at home.** Luke opens with Zechariah and Mary, both familiar from Q 3 and 19. John states its purpose: "that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (John 20:31, ESV).
3. **Let him meet Jesus there.** Jesus forgives sins on his own authority (Mark 2:5–12). He lays down his life and takes it up again (John 10:17–18). Thomas calls him "My Lord and my God" (John 20:28).
4. **Show the Father who gives his Son.** The Qur'an says God is no one's father and does not love unbelievers (Q 5:18; 3:32). The Gospel says, "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son" (John 3:16, ESV), and "while we were still sinners, Christ died for us" (Rom. 5:8, ESV; Luke 6:35).
5. **Offer assurance instead of the scales.** The Qur'an weighs deeds on scales at the judgment (Q 7:8–9; 21:47). In Christ, God is both just and the justifier (Rom. 3:26), so salvation is a gift, not a wage (Eph. 2:8–9): "that you may know that you have eternal life" (1 John 5:13, ESV).
6. **Keep at it in love.** Pray for him (Rom. 10:1). Stay his friend after the argument. One plants, another waters, and God gives the growth (1 Cor. 3:6–7; 2 Tim. 2:25).
