## The Suffering Servant: Jewish responses

| # | They say | Where seen | Reply and tactic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Isaiah names Israel as God's servant six times before chapter 53." | [Jews for Judaism](https://www.jewsforjudaism.org/knowledge/videos/who-does-god-say-is-his-servant) | Isaiah also has a servant who restores Israel (49:5–6). "Can the one who bears Israel's sin be the Israel who sinned?" (Sibling Rivalry) |
| 2 | "Read from 52:7. The kings of the nations are speaking, shocked at Israel's redemption." | [Jews for Judaism](https://jewsforjudaism.org/knowledge/videos/the-nations-shut-their-mouth-in-disbelief); Tovia Singer ([podcast](https://metacast.app/podcast/torah-truths-with-rabbi-tovia-singer/3FOkJAg9/isaiah-53-part-1/8JdCdbxT)) | "Whom does God call 'my people' in 52:4–6?" Whoever speaks, the servant is innocent and dies for others. (Sibling Rivalry) |
| 3 | "53:8 says 'for the sins of my people they were punished.' Christians changed 'them' to 'him.'" | Tovia Singer, quoted on the [Ehrman forum](https://ehrmanblog.org/forum/the-hebrew-bible/problem-with-the-suffering-servant-translation-who-is-the-suffering-servant-isaiah-53-rabbi-tovia-singer/) | *Lamo* can be singular (Isa. 44:15). (Just the Facts) |
| 4 | "'No deceit in his mouth' describes the righteous remnant (Zeph. 3:13)." | Tovia Singer, as quoted by [Eitan Bar](https://eitan.bar/articles/rabbi-tovia-singer-isaiah-53/) | "Is the remnant sinless? Does it confess sins on Yom Kippur?" (Sibling Rivalry) |
| 5 | "'Seed' always means physical children, and long life can't fit a divine Messiah." | Tovia Singer, as quoted by Eitan Bar | "A seed of evildoers" (Isa. 1:4) is figurative. One cut off and buried who then prolongs his days has risen. (Columbo 1: "What do you mean by 'always'?") |
| 6 | "Ezekiel 18 forbids the innocent dying for the guilty, and human sacrifice is an abomination." | Tovia Singer, as quoted by Eitan Bar | The Israel reading has innocent Israel suffering for the nations: the same problem. (Sibling Rivalry) |
| 7 | "The 'forbidden chapter' is a missionary myth." | [Jews for Judaism](https://jewsforjudaism.org/knowledge/videos/unraveling-the-truth-about-the-so-called-forbidden-chapter); [Israel Bible Center](https://weekly.israelbiblecenter.com/was-isaiah-53-forbidden) | Agree that deliberate removal is not shown. "Have you read it? Shall we read it now?" |
| 8 | "The prevailing rabbinic interpretation is that the servant is Israel." | [Outreach Judaism](https://outreachjudaism.org/tag/isaiah-53/) | "What did the Targum and the Talmud say?" (Rhodes Scholar) |
| 9 | "Isaiah 53 is about a future Messiah, not Jesus." | Yahya A. Sharif, [Times of Israel blog](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/isaiah-53-points-to-a-future-messiah-not-jesus/) | Agree it is an individual. "Who was cut off before the Temple fell (Dan. 9:26), buried with the rich, and raised?" (Columbo 3) |
| 10 | "Nobody died for my sins. Because I am accountable for my own actions." A message that someone died for you takes away accountability. | Shmuley Boteach vs Michael Brown, "Did Jesus Die for Our Sins?", New York, 2007, in the edit aired on Brown's program, which omits the cross-examination ([~14:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=886s)). Seen once | Brown's answer: forgiveness through his death comes with repentance, never in place of it. Jesus began with "repent ye, and believe the gospel" (Mark 1:15), and God "commandeth all men every where to repent" (Acts 17:30). Ask: "Have you ever needed mercy for what you did?" (Columbo 1) |
| 11 | Numbers 35: take no ransom for a murderer, and blood is atoned for only by the blood of the one who shed it. | Boteach, opening ([~18:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=1134s)). Seen once | The same chapter frees the manslayer at the high priest's death: he stays in the city of refuge "unto the death of the high priest" (Num. 35:25, 28). The Talmud asks what atones there and answers: the high priest's death (Makkot 11b, cited by Brown). (Turnabout; Just the Facts) |
| 12 | "Isaiah 53 has always been understood by the Jews to refer to the nation of Israel." Origen recorded it in the third century. | Boteach, opening ([~19:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=1180s)). Seen once; compare row 8 | He is right that Origen met the Israel reading (*Contra Celsum* 1.55), so don't say it began with Rashi. But Origen answered it from 53:8: if the people are the subject, how is the servant stricken for the iniquity of God's people? And the reading was never the only one: the Talmud reads the passage of the Messiah (Sanhedrin 98b; Brown). (Just the Facts) |
| 13 | The chapter changes tenses, past, present and future, about one subject. That fits an eternal nation, not one man. | Boteach, opening ([~19:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=1195s)). Seen once | Brown's answer: the tenses fit one man whose suffering is past, whose work goes on, and whose exaltation is still to come. He is cut off, yet "he shall prolong his days" (Isa. 53:8, 10). (Columbo 1) |
| 14 | "Despised and rejected"? The Gospels say great crowds followed Jesus and shouted hosanna. | Boteach, opening ([~20:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=1245s)), citing Matt. 4:25 and 21:8–9. Seen once | The crowds were brief. The same city soon cried "Let him be crucified" (Matt. 27:22), and "his own received him not" (John 1:11). Most of his own people still reject him. (Just the Facts) |
| 15 | The servant "did not open his mouth," but Jesus cried with a loud voice on the cross. | Boteach, opening and rebuttal ([~22:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=1358s); ~42:03). Seen once | Isaiah speaks of the trial: "he is brought as a lamb to the slaughter" (Isa. 53:7). Before his accusers Jesus "answered him to never a word" (Matt. 27:14; Mark 15:5). His cry from the cross is Psalm 22:1. (Just the Facts) |
| 16 | Verse 10 says God willed to crush him, but Christians teach that Jesus chose to lay down his life: "It was not God's plan, it was his plan." | Boteach, opening and rebuttal ([~22:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=1327s); ~43:41). Seen once | Scripture says both. Jesus said, "I lay it down of myself" (John 10:18), and he was delivered "by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God" (Acts 2:23). The Father and the Son willed it together. (Sibling Rivalry, answered) |
| 17 | 53:10 reads "if his soul would acknowledge guilt," and "in this entire chapter of Isaiah 53, there's no mention of atonement." A righteous man suffers for others' sins, but no one can atone for yours. | Boteach, rebuttal ([~43:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=2637s); ~45:01). Seen once | The word is *asham*, the Torah's guilt offering (Lev. 5:15; 7:1); the King James reads "make his soul an offering for sin" (Isa. 53:10). The chapter ends "he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors" (Isa. 53:12), as the Day of Atonement goat "shall bear upon him all their iniquities" (Lev. 16:22). (Just the Facts) This escape gets past SOP exposing question 2; see loop/harvest-log.md. |
| 18 | Forty years after this atonement, Rome killed about a million Jews and destroyed the Temple. "Is that atonement?" | Boteach, rebuttal ([~38:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=2325s)). Seen once. Said with heat before an audience; across a table, meet the grief first | Jesus wept over the city and foretold its fall "because thou knewest not the time of thy visitation" (Luke 19:44). Daniel too puts the cutting off of the anointed one before the city's destruction (Dan. 9:26). His death is received by repentance and faith (Acts 20:21; Rom. 3:25), not by birth. (Just the Facts) |
| 19 | Isaiah 53 fits Abraham Lincoln, Martin Luther King and Moses: anyone who suffers for doing right. Just not Jesus. | Boteach, opening ([~26:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=1583s)). Seen once | Ask which of them "had done no violence, neither was any deceit in his mouth" (Isa. 53:9), was cut off and then prolonged his days (53:8, 10), and bore "the sin of many" (53:12). (Columbo 3) |
| 20 | Jesus was a Torah-observant Pharisee, "and he never taught that he would die for anyone's sins." | Boteach, opening ([~18:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqPANglCOZo&t=1117s)). Seen once | Jesus said he came "to give his life a ransom for many" (Mark 10:45), called the cup his blood "shed for many for the remission of sins" (Matt. 26:28), and said "the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep" (John 10:11). (Just the Facts) |
| 21 | *Answer to an invitation:* "My rabbi told me not to read Christian material." Pressed, he adds: "I trust him. He is very smart and studies all the time." | An Israeli man near Jerusalem's Temple Mount, offered the written testimony of a Jewish father who came to faith in Jesus, as a Life in Messiah staff member reports, undated ([Life in Messiah](https://www.lifeinmessiah.org/blog/my-rabbi-told-me-not-to)). Seen once | Don't set him against his rabbi, and don't press the book. Ask to read his own Scriptures with him: "Would you read Isaiah 53 with me from your Tanakh?" Moses told all Israel that the word is near them, to hear it and do it (Deut. 30:11–14), and the Jews of Berea searched the Scriptures for themselves (Acts 17:10–11). (The bridge to Christ, A′) |
| 22 | *Answer to an invitation:* "I have been really curious about the New Testament. But it's forbidden to read it. I want to, but I am not allowed." His last word: "I will ask him about this, but I don't think he will like it." They part with a smile and a handshake; he had declined the book. | The same man, later in the same conversation ([Life in Messiah](https://www.lifeinmessiah.org/blog/my-rabbi-told-me-not-to)). Seen once | A guarded maybe: leave it open and unhurried. Tell him the New Testament is a Jewish book about the covenant promised to "the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah" (Jer. 31:31), and that you would gladly read it with him when he is free to. Don't press: Paul planted and Apollos watered, "but God gave the increase" (1 Cor. 3:6). (The bridge to Christ, A′) |
