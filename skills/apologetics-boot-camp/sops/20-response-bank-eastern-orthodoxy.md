## Thou Shalt Not Bow Down: Eastern Orthodox responses

| # | They say | Where seen | Reply and tactic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "We don't worship icons. Worship (*latreia*) is for God alone; icons get veneration (*proskynesis*)." | Nicaea II, Definition; [St. Andrew GOC](https://saintandrewgoc.org/blog/2019/4/9/the-orthodox-vocabulary-in-worship); [GreekReporter](https://greekreporter.com/2025/12/16/orthodox-christians-icons-church/) | Grant the difference. "Which of the two does Exodus 20:5 forbid?" It forbids both: *proskynēseis* and *latreusēs*. (Sibling Rivalry) |
| 2 | "The bow before the icon is the material form of a worship that flowers into *latreia* of Christ. English uses 'worship' loosely." | [St. Andrew GOC](https://saintandrewgoc.org/blog/2019/4/9/the-orthodox-vocabulary-in-worship) | "Then you do bow to the image. What does the commandment say about bowing to images?" Read Ex. 20:5. Leave the English aside; the word in their Greek is *proskynesis*. (Just the Facts) |
| 3 | "The honor passes to the prototype." | Nicaea II, Definition, from Basil, *On the Holy Spirit* 18.45 | "Aaron's feast was 'to the LORD.' Did the honor pass to the LORD, or did God say 'they have worshipped it'?" (Ex. 32:5, 8). (Taking the Roof Off) |
| 4 | "An idol is an image of a false god. The commandment forbids idols, not icons of the true God." | [Fr. John Whiteford, Pravmir](https://www.pravmir.com/?p=281719); Nicaea II, as summarized in [*Christian History*](https://christianitytoday.com/history/issues/issue-54/kissers-and-smashers.html) | The commandment covers "any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above," and the calf stood for the LORD (Ex. 20:4; 32:4–5). "In your Septuagint, what word does Exodus 20:4 use for the image?" *Eidōlon*. (Just the Facts) |
| 5 | "God commanded the cherubim and the tabernacle images in the same book. So the commandment doesn't forbid all images." | [Ancient Faith, *Our Life in Christ*](https://www.ancientfaith.com/podcasts/ourlife/icons_in_the_orthodox_faith_part_1/); [Orthodox Bridge](https://blogs.ancientfaith.com/orthodoxbridge/early-jewish-attitudes-toward-images/) | Agreed: images aren't the sin. "Did God ever command anyone to bow to the cherubim or kiss them?" They were hidden in the Most Holy Place (Lev. 16:2). (Burden of proof) |
| 6 | "God worked miracles through the ark and the bronze serpent." | [12 FAQs about Icons](https://catalog.obitel-minsk.com/blog/2017/05/12-frequently-asked-questions-abou) | Yes, and when Israel burned incense to the serpent, Hezekiah broke it (2 Kings 18:4). "Your church burns incense to icons. What makes the difference?" (Sibling Rivalry) |
| 7 | "Kissing an icon is no different from kissing a photo of my family." | [Pillars of Truth](https://medium.com/pillars-of-truth/holy-icons-and-the-anti-icons-211a3d6fd94); [Ancient Faith, *Our Life in Christ*](https://www.ancientfaith.com/podcasts/ourlife/icons_and_veneration) | "Does anyone burn incense to the photo, or curse those who won't kiss it?" Nicaea II does both for icons. Scripture names the kiss as idolatrous homage (Hos. 13:2; 1 Kings 19:18). (Taking the Roof Off) |
| 8 | "Pious Jews kiss the mezuzah and the Torah scroll. Kissing holy things is biblical." | [Fr. John Whiteford, Pravmir](https://www.pravmir.com/?p=281719); [Ancient Faith, *Our Life in Christ*](https://www.ancientfaith.com/podcasts/ourlife/icons_and_veneration) | A scroll is not a likeness of anything. "Where does Scripture approve kissing an image?" It names the kiss to an image as idolatry (Hos. 13:2). (Burden of proof) |
| 9 | "Now that God has become visible in Christ, he can be depicted, and the image venerated." | John of Damascus, *On the Divine Images* 1.4, 1.8; [Orthodox Bridge](https://blogs.ancientfaith.com/orthodoxbridge/cracks-reformed-iconoclasm/) | God was seen in form before Sinai (Gen. 32:30), yet the ban stood. After the incarnation John still says, "keep yourselves from idols" (1 John 5:20–21). And the question is the bow, not the picture. (Staying on topic) |
| 10 | "Icons are windows to heaven; they protect us from idolatry. We bow before the icon of Christ, seeing through it him and his Father." | [Pravoslavie.ru, "No Graven Image"](https://pravoslavie.ru/69053.html) | "So you do bow before the image. Where does God command that?" The pagans also said they worshipped the unseen power through the statue, not the statue (Augustine, *Expositions of the Psalms*, Ps. 113 [115], sermon 2). (Burden of proof) |
| 11 | "The Seventh Ecumenical Council settled this. Who are you to overturn it?" | [*Christian History*](https://christianitytoday.com/history/issues/issue-54/kissers-and-smashers.html); Nicaea II acclamations | "Your council says it agrees with Scripture. Shall we test that at the text it cites?" Don't argue authority. Return to Ex. 20:5. (Staying on topic) |
| 12 | "Joshua fell on his face before the ark." | Josh. 7:6; John of Damascus, as summarized by Schooping, *Disillusioned*, Part II, ch. 3 (veneration of the Temple) | The ark was the throne of the LORD, "which dwelleth between the cherubims" (1 Sam. 4:4), not a likeness. The bow was to God present. (Just the Facts) |
| 13 | "Jacob venerated the top of Joseph's staff" (Heb. 11:21). | John of Damascus, as summarized by Schooping, *Disillusioned*, Part II, ch. 3 | He "worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff"; Genesis says he "bowed himself upon the bed's head" (Gen. 47:31), as David did in prayer (1 Kings 1:47). (Just the Facts) |
| 14 | "Your churches have crosses and pictures too. You're iconoclasts in theory only." | [Orthodox Bridge](https://blogs.ancientfaith.com/orthodoxbridge/cracks-reformed-iconoclasm/) | Having an image isn't the sin; bowing to it is (Lev. 26:1). "If we bowed to ours, wouldn't the same commandment judge us?" (Turnabout) |
| 15 | "The early church had images: the catacombs, Dura-Europos." | [Orthodox Bridge](https://blogs.ancientfaith.com/orthodoxbridge/early-jewish-attitudes-toward-images/) | Paintings on a wall are not bowing to them. Don't debate history; return to the bow (Ex. 20:5). (Staying on topic) |
| 16 | "It is simply wrong to say the second commandment forbids honoring icons. We don't see icons as deities, but as reminders." | Fr. Ambrose, [sermon on the Second Commandment](https://www.theotokos-skete.org/files/Fr%20Ambrose%20Files/Fr%20Ambrose%20Sunday%20Sermons/Jan9_2011Secondof10Commandments.pdf), Jan. 9, 2011 | Grant that he doesn't think the icon is a god. Neither did Aaron think the calf was. "What did God call their bow?" (Ex. 32:8). (Taking the Roof Off) |
| 17 | "So you think a billion Orthodox Christians are idolaters? We've done this for 1,200 years." Then he presses Mary, the saints and the council in one breath. | Not yet recorded: a composite for the steamroller drill. Replace with a recorded line when one is found | Stop him gently: "May I answer your first point before we go to the next?" Then return to the one question: does the name of the bow change what Ex. 20:5 forbids? (Steamroller) |
