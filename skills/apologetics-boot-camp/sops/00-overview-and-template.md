# Apologetics SOPs: Methods for Engaging Other Religions

Nov 22, 2026 · @Faa Diallo

## Purpose and how to use

Each tab is a standard operating procedure (SOP) for answering one religion or cult from Scripture. Each SOP has its own name, and every tab follows the same five-part template, so a worker who knows one SOP can use them all.

**Why we do this.** Christ's people are to give a defense of their hope (1 Pet. 3:15), to contend for the faith delivered once for all (Jude 3), and to take every thought captive to obey Christ (2 Cor. 10:5).

**How we do it.** "The Lord's servant must not be quarrelsome but kind to everyone, able to teach, patiently enduring evil, correcting his opponents with gentleness" (2 Tim. 2:24–25, ESV; 1 Pet. 3:15; Col. 4:6). The goal is the person's repentance and knowledge of the truth, not winning the exchange (2 Tim. 2:25–26; 1 Cor. 9:22).

**No neutral ground.** Every method here stands on Scripture. Some methods argue inside the other side's own authority to show it fails by its own standard. That is answering a fool according to his folly without becoming like him (Prov. 26:4–5), as Paul argued from the Athenians' own altar and poets (Acts 17:22–28; Titus 1:12–13).

**The war we are in.** These SOPs are orders for a real war, as the New Testament describes it. "The weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but have divine power to destroy strongholds. We destroy arguments and every lofty opinion raised against the knowledge of God, and take every thought captive to obey Christ" (2 Cor. 10:4–5, ESV). Three things follow.

- **The enemy is not the person.** "We do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but… against the spiritual forces of evil" (Eph. 6:12, ESV). The Muslim or Witness is a captive to be freed, held "in the snare of the devil" (2 Tim. 2:26; 2 Cor. 4:4). We aim at the stronghold, the false teaching and the arguments that hold it up, never at the man.
- **Our weapon is the Word, and our supply line is prayer.** The only blade in the armor is "the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God," and the armor passage ends in prayer "at all times" (Eph. 6:17–18). Every SOP is waged with Scripture and in prayer.
- **The soldier is an ambassador.** Paul closes the armor passage calling himself "an ambassador in chains" (Eph. 6:20). The same letter that says "share in suffering as a good soldier" says the Lord's servant must correct opponents "with gentleness" (2 Tim. 2:3, 24–25). War and diplomacy are not two models here; they are one calling. The war ends in an offer of peace: "be reconciled to God" (2 Cor. 5:20).

**Using an SOP.**

- **Short version** (top of each tab): one phone screen to carry into a live conversation.
- **Long version** (the rest of the tab): learn it before the conversation. It explains every step and shows its support.
- Scripture is quoted from the ESV. Each tab names the edition it uses for the other group's texts.

**Methodology.** Every SOP is carried out by one method, Questioning Them to Death, adapted from Gregory Koukl's *Tactics*. We lead with questions until the false view gives way. See Methodology: Questioning Them to Death.

## The standard template

Every SOP opens with a header and a short version. The long version then runs through five sections. The first and last sections mirror each other: we begin from Scripture and end by turning the person to Christ. The method sits at the center.

| Part | What it holds | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Header | SOP name, group, main sources, editions quoted, last reviewed | One block at the top |
| Short version | The argument in one sentence, a prayer before engaging, the questions to ask in order (ending with the question that exposes the flaw), and what to avoid | One phone screen; nothing the long version doesn't support |
| A. Foundation | What Scripture says about this group's error and how we should carry ourselves toward them | Scripture only, quoted in the ESV |
| B. Know them | Background for beginners, their beliefs, final authority, view of the Bible, and terms they use differently | Stated as they would state it |
| C. The method | Why this approach was chosen, the argument as numbered premises, the witnesses for each, the conversation steps, ground rules, and a question plan using the Questioning Them to Death method | Each premise has two or three witnesses, from their own authority where the method is internal |
| B′. Their answers | Each common objection, the reply, its witnesses, and pitfalls | Each reply has two or three witnesses |
| A′. Turn to Christ | Where to take the conversation once the method has done its work | Ends in the Gospel, quoted in the ESV |

**Witnesses.** Every premise and every reply rests on at least two independent witnesses. A Scripture passage counts once. Their own scripture counts as a witness against them in an internal method. A claim with only one witness is marked as such. In training and in conversation, call these supporting verses, or support, so they are never confused with Jehovah's Witnesses.
