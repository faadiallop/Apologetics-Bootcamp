## Training plan: Borrowed Ground

Source SOP: Atheists. Bank rows refer to the Borrowed Ground table in the Response Bank.

| Stage | Skill | Drill | Opponent | Pass when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Foundation | Knowledge | Coach teaches, then checks: the argument in one sentence; why no neutral ground (Rom. 1:18–21; Ps. 19:1–4); Prov. 26:4–5; why we don't call him a fool (Matt. 5:22) | None | What the passages say, and other facts, right in substance |
| 2. Premises | Knowledge | Learn, then recite the four things he uses (logic, reason, order of nature, morality), with two witnesses each for Premise 2 (his view can't account for it) and two verses each for Premise 3 (God does) | None | Two correct witnesses for every point, from memory (references not required) |
| 3. Gather | Columbo step 1 (ch. 4) | "What do you mean by atheist? What's ultimately real? Where do right and wrong come from?" Questions only, four turns | Casual unbeliever; rows 1, 20 | His view restated accurately ("So everything is matter and energy…"), with no statements made |
| 4. Burden of proof | Columbo step 2 (ch. 5) | "How did you come to that conclusion? What would count as evidence for God?" Refuse to carry the whole burden for "lack of belief" | Online atheist; rows 1, 13, 14 | Burden stays on whoever made the claim every time, and he asks for the reasons behind each main claim (not every passing remark) |
| 5. Rescues | Professor's ploy; hot seat; staying on topic: let it pass, park it, or narrate (ch. 6) | Philosophy student piles on rows 5, 7, 10 and 11; trainee switches to fact-finding and says "Let me think about it." Online atheist drifts to Christian wars, hell or Bible "contradictions"; trainee honors it, parks it, and returns to the one question | Philosophy student; Online atheist | No burden taken on; composure kept; back on the question within two turns, by the way back that fits |
| 6. Lead | Columbo step 3 (ch. 7) | Run one line of the Question plan in SOP Section C (logic, reason, order of nature or morality) to its end; the coach varies which | Casual unbeliever, then Philosophy student | He says in his own words that he uses the thing and his view doesn't supply it, or gives one of the recorded escapes |
| 7. Find the flaw | Suicide; Sibling Rivalry; Infanticide; Roof; Steamroller; Facts (chs. 10–16) | Opponent fires one response; trainee says to the coach what the flaw is, in his own words or by name, then asks the exposing question. When the online atheist steamrolls, he uses stop, shame, leave | Any persona; rows 13 (Suicide), 18 and 20 (Sibling Rivalry), 2 and 12 (Infanticide), 4 and 7 (Roof), 8 (Facts) | Right flaw seen (described truly; its name not required) and exposed by a question, 4 of 5; composure kept |

| 9. Full conversations | All | About 15 turns, opener to A′ (offer to read John 1 together) | Casual unbeliever, then Online atheist, then Philosophy student, then Ex-Christian, then Humanist chaplain, then Seasoned debater | Pass on the rubric with each persona, in order; he meets the next only after passing the one before. After the third, he is street ready |

**Exposing the flaws (SOP Section C).** In Stage 1 the trainee maps every answer to each question in the SOP's "Exposing the flaws" table and what it costs. From Stage 6 on, every round must end with one of those questions, asked only after the view is pinned down, followed by silence. The coach marks *wisdom* down if it comes too early, if the trainee answers it himself, or if he runs more than one of the four lines in a round. The opponent must answer as real people do: concede, dodge, or change the subject. Never invent an escape the bank doesn't record.

**The ex-Christian.** With this persona, the coach marks *character* down if the trainee argues before asking what happened, or answers grief with an argument (Rom. 12:15). The argument still comes, but after listening.

**The bridge to Christ (SOP Section A′).** This is drilled only in Stage 8, after the method is passed, so that he learns one thing at a time. Until then a round ends when the opponent answers the exposing question, and the coach neither asks for, hints at nor scores the bridge or the gospel; if the trainee crosses before the round's goal is met, the opponent answers in a sentence and the round closes. Each round still ends with his closing turns, his chance to point to Christ in a sentence (step 3 of a round, above). In Stage 8 he first tells the gospel aloud with no opponent: who Jesus is, our sin, his death for sinners and his rising, and the call to repent and believe, true to Scripture and in his own words (references not required). Then each round opens just after the exposing question, and the opponent either gives the costly answer or dodges; the coach varies it. The trainee crosses to Christ within two turns: after a concession, with the SOP's bridge question; after a dodge, by parking the point and giving the reason for his hope. When he asks leave to tell the gospel, the member gives him room or closes the door, and the coach varies this too ("Go on, I'm listening," "So what would I have to do?", "I've got to run"). The coach marks *wisdom* down if he keeps arguing instead of crossing, stops at "some god exists," or presses the gospel after the door has closed, and *knowledge* down if what he says of it is false or would mislead, or if he had room and left out who Jesus is, sin, the cross and resurrection, or the call to repent and believe. A short word that fits a closing door, with a Gospel left behind, loses nothing. Pass: crossed within two turns, 4 of 5 rounds.

**After passing.** Run one full conversation a month with a random persona, and add every new response you meet to the bank.
