**SOP questions an escape got past**

1. **Islam, exposing question 4 (Q 10:94).** Haqiqatjou answered that the earlier scripture is "a data point… not the ultimate arbiter of truth" (row 28), a third answer the question doesn't price. Proposed addition to its "Every answer costs" cell: *"Only a data point":* then, on the matters the doubter asks about, a Qur'an that disagreed with it would give him reason to doubt, as Haqiqatjou granted (~59:33–1:00:06); and those books say Jesus was crucified. Support: Q 10:94; Q 5:47; the four Gospels' crucifixion accounts.
2. **Jewish, exposing question 2.** The question assumes 53:10 calls the servant's life a guilt offering. Boteach read it "if his soul would acknowledge guilt" and said the chapter never mentions atonement (row 17). Proposed addition: *Recorded escape,* "53:10 says 'acknowledge guilt,' and there's no atonement in the chapter" (Boteach): the word is *asham*, the guilt offering of Lev. 5:15 and 7:1, and the chapter ends "he bare the sin of many, and made intercession for the transgressors" (Isa. 53:12).
3. **Jewish, exposing question 3 and the B′ reply to "Rashi says it is Israel."** The reply implies the Israel reading began with Rashi. Boteach cited Origen, who met it in the third century (row 12; *Contra Celsum* 1.55, checked). Proposed reply: "The Israel reading is old: Origen met it in the third century. But Origen answered it from 53:8, and the Targum and the Talmud read the Messiah here." Support: Origen, *Contra Celsum* 1.55; Targum Jonathan on 52:13; Sanhedrin 98b.
4. **Atheist, exposing question 1.** The cost for *"In our minds"* says the laws would differ by brain. Barker granted there was no logic before minds but said it is universal "within our species" (row 42). Proposed addition: *"Universal within our species":* then it binds no mind but man's, and his charge that God contradicts himself reaches no further than human brains (Ayala, ~50:22). Ask: "Before there were brains, could a thing both be and not be?"
5. **Atheist, exposing question 4.** To *"Truly evil… where does the standard come from?"* Barker answered that harm is "an objective measurable standard" (row 45). Proposed addition: *"Harm is the standard":* measuring harm tells how much, not why causing it is wrong. Ask: "Why ought anyone to lessen harm, if he defines morality another way?" Support: Rom. 2:14–15; Ayala, ~1:44:47.
6. **Atheist, all four exposing questions.** "We are not debating worldviews today" (row 44) declines every question at once, and no cell answers a refusal to engage. Proposed ground rule: "If he says where logic or morality comes from is off the topic, show that it is the topic: his own charges use logic and a moral standard." Support: Prov. 26:4–5; Rom. 1:19–21; Ayala vs Barker (~33:40, ~44:16).
