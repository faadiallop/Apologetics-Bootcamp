# Live round: the coach drills his hard tactic

**Guards:** "Each session," "Working on what he finds hardest" (mark every chance; drill it; three or more chances), "A practice conversation, in voice," "Hints in the middle of a conversation," and "Recording" (save as you go).

## Setup

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Group | Atheists (Borrowed Ground) |
| Trainee | Daniel, not a beginner, on voice |
| His card | The card in `skills/apologetics-boot-camp/evals/marks-missed-requests-for-reasons/files/training-log.md`: his hard tactic is step 2, asking for reasons |
| Length | Two practice conversations, each followed by coaching |

## The simulated trainee

Give the trainee agent these instructions and nothing else. It must not see the skill or its materials.

> You are Daniel, a Christian who has talked with atheists before. You are training by voice, so say everything as you would aloud.
>
> Open with: "Let's do boot camp. Atheists again. I'm on voice."
>
> Do what the coach asks before the practice begins. If it asks you to plan your questions aloud, give two short questions.
>
> In a practice conversation, when the atheist makes a claim, answer it yourself with a counter-argument of one or two sentences. Do this two times in three. The third time, ask, "What makes you say that?"
>
> When the coach calls time out and tells you what to do, do exactly that on your retry. Later in the conversation, fall back into your habit.
>
> Stay polite. Never mock. Stop after the coaching that follows your second practice conversation.

## What the grader checks

The grader sees only the transcript, the training log before and after, and these statements.

1. The coach reads Daniel's card and begins with his hard tactic, asking for reasons. It does not introduce the training again.
2. Before the first conversation it tells him to pray, asks him to recall when the tactic is called for and the question it asks, and has him say aloud the questions he plans to ask.
3. It sets up each conversation in a sentence or two: who he is talking to, the tactic, and about how many turns.
4. In each conversation the atheist gives him at least three chances at the tactic, each a claim recorded for atheists in the materials.
5. Every turn the atheist speaks is one to three sentences in everyday words, with no stage directions, lists, headings or bracketed citations.
6. The coach calls time out only for a miss on the drilled tactic or a move that would lose the person, and never more than once in three of Daniel's turns.
7. Each time-out is two or three spoken sentences: it quotes the atheist's line, says in plain words what to do, and has Daniel try the turn again.
8. After each conversation it coaches in under a minute: one thing done well, the tactic as the thing to change with his own words quoted, his count, and what comes next.
9. The count the coach gives for each conversation agrees with the grader's own count of chances and hits, within one.
10. The training log gains a new card for Daniel after the first conversation, above his older card, and that card is brought up to date after the second.
11. Daniel is not passed on the tactic, and the coach does not coach how he tells the gospel or crosses to it. Each round ends with the atheist showing he is about to leave, and the coach does not announce the closing turns. Each time he lets them pass without pointing to Christ, the coaching reminds him, and nothing is scored for it.
