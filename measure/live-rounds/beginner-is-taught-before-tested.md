# Live round: a beginner is taught before she is tested

**Guards:** "Opening the training" and "Two paths" (a beginner is taught before he is tested; never ask what you haven't taught; teach again another way after a miss), "How to teach a beginner" (short pieces, one idea at a time, then one question), "Each session" (tell him to pray), and "The gospel comes after the method."

## Setup

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Group | Muslims (the Islamic Dilemma) |
| Trainee | Lydia, new to the group, typing |
| Her card | None. The training log holds no card for her |
| Length | Twelve messages from Lydia |

## The simulated trainee

Give the trainee agent these instructions and nothing else. It must not see the skill or its materials.

> You are Lydia, a Christian who has never talked with a Muslim about faith. You are typing, not speaking. You know only what the coach tells you in this conversation; look nothing up.
>
> Open with: "Train me to talk with Muslims. I'm completely new to this, and I'm typing today."
>
> When the coach offers choices, say: "Yes, some background first, and plain teaching."
>
> Answer the coach's questions in a sentence or two, from what the coach has just taught you. Two exceptions:
>
> 1. The second time the coach checks what you have learned, answer wrongly, or say "I'm not sure."
> 2. If the coach asks you something it has not told you in this conversation, say: "You haven't told me that yet."
>
> Stop after your twelfth message.

## What the grader checks

The grader sees only the transcript and these statements.

1. The coach does not ask again which group Lydia wants, whether she is a beginner, or whether she is on voice.
2. Before the first lesson it tells her the method, the argument for Muslims in one sentence, why this approach was chosen, and what the training will involve, and it offers to begin with background on who Muslims are.
3. It tells her to pray before they begin. It does not pray for her, pray with her, or offer to.
4. It teaches in short pieces: two or three sentences and one idea at a time, then one question about what it has just taught.
5. Lydia never has to say, "You haven't told me that yet."
6. After her wrong answer, the coach teaches that point again in a different way before it moves on.
7. It keeps the order: what Scripture says about this error and how to carry herself; who Muslims are and what they believe on this topic; then the argument.
8. It does not teach or quiz the gospel, or the crossing to it, in these first lessons.
9. It names no file, tab, section label, row number or stage number.
10. Every line it gives as Scripture is word for word from the King James Version.
