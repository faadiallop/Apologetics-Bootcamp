# Live round: the coach keeps him on the one question

**Guards:** "Staying on topic" (name the one question; throw in side issues from the group's material; score it), the rescues in the table of tactics, and "Hints in the middle of a conversation" (pause for a burden he never claimed).

## Setup

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Group | Muslims (the Islamic Dilemma) |
| Trainee | Silas, not a beginner, on voice |
| His card | Hard tactic: the rescues (refusing a burden he never took on, and staying on topic). Steps 1 and 2 are cleared |
| Length | One practice conversation of about six turns, then coaching |

His card, to place in the training log before the round:

```
## Silas · Muslims (the Islamic Dilemma)

### Boot camp log · Nov 18, 2026 · third session (voice)

**Next: the rescues, starting with a burden he never took on.**

**What he learned.** He has made no claim, so he owes no proof. A side issue gets one sentence, then a question that brings the talk back.

**How he did.** Refusing a burden he never took on: 0 of 2, 1 of 3, 1 of 2 over his last three conversations. Staying on topic: he followed three side issues out of four past one sentence.

**Hard tactics, in the book's order.** The rescues; then step 3, leading with questions.

**Cleared:** step 1, asking what the other person means; step 2, asking for reasons.

**Passages to review.** Qur'an 5:47; 10:94.
```

## The simulated trainee

Give the trainee agent these instructions and nothing else. It must not see the skill or its materials.

> You are Silas, a Christian who has talked with Muslims at a street stall. You are training by voice, so say everything as you would aloud.
>
> Open with: "Boot camp, Muslims again. I'm on voice."
>
> Do what the coach asks before the practice begins.
>
> In the practice conversation you have two habits. Whenever the Muslim challenges you to prove something, try to prove it, at length. Whenever he raises another subject, such as the Trinity, hadith or wars fought by Christians, follow it and answer it for two or three sentences.
>
> When the coach calls time out and tells you what to do, do exactly that on your retry. Then fall back into your habits.
>
> Stay polite. Never mock. Stop after the coaching that follows the conversation.

## What the grader checks

The grader sees only the transcript, the training log before and after, and these statements.

1. Before the conversation the coach names the one question the conversation is about.
2. As the Muslim, it raises two or three side issues taken from the materials for Muslims, at least one of them emotional.
3. As the Muslim, it shifts the burden onto Silas at least once, with a response the materials record.
4. When Silas takes up a burden he never claimed, or follows a side issue past one sentence, the coach calls time out and quotes the Muslim's line, but never more than once in three of Silas's turns.
5. Every turn the Muslim speaks is one to three sentences in everyday words, with no stage directions, lists, headings or bracketed citations.
6. After the conversation the coach gives his on-topic score: the side issues raised, those he followed past one sentence, and the turns he took to return.
7. Silas is not passed on the rescues.
8. The training log gains a new card for Silas above his older card.
